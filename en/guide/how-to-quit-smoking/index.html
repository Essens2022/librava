How to Quit Smoking: A Kind Step by Step Plan From Quit Day On

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you have tried to quit smoking before and ended up back at the shop, you are not weak and you have not failed: you have practiced. The best way to quit smoking is to set a quit date one to two weeks away, get free support, use a quit aid such as combination nicotine replacement, and have a plan ready for every craving and trigger.

Support plus a quit aid gives you a much better chance than willpower alone. This guide walks you through each step, from choosing your date to getting through the first 72 hours, handling withdrawal and bouncing back from a slip. It works for cigarettes, roll-ups (rollies) and vapes alike.

Why quitting smoking is so hard

Quitting is hard because you are dealing with three things at once. There is the nicotine addiction, which makes your body feel restless and uncomfortable when levels drop. There is the habit, the cigarette with the first coffee, after dinner or in the car. And there is the emotional side, when smoking has become how you handle stress, boredom or a bad day.

It also helps to know where the harm really comes from. Nicotine is what keeps you hooked, but most of the damage from smoking comes from the tar, carbon monoxide and other chemicals in burned tobacco smoke.

A few myths worth dropping:

  • Light or mild cigarettes are safer. People tend to smoke them harder to get the same nicotine.
  • Cutting down for good is enough. Without a fixed end date, numbers usually drift back up.
  • It is too late for me. It is never too late to benefit from quitting, at any age.

Pick your quit date and method

There are two main ways to quit, and both can work.

MethodHow it worksSuits you if
Stopping on one dayYou prepare, then stop completely on your quit dateYou like a clear line, or cutting down has drifted back before
Cutting down to quitYou reduce over a few weeks to a fixed quit dateStopping in one go makes you so anxious you keep putting it off

For most people, stopping on one day is the most straightforward, and studies suggest it works at least as well as cutting down first. If you cut down, the end date has to be real.

Choose a fairly ordinary, low-stress day one to two weeks from now. Avoid big deadlines, weddings, house moves and heavy drinking events.

Before quit day

  • Tell a few people, and say exactly what helps: do not offer me any, and forgive me if I am snappy.
  • The night before, clear out every cigarette, lighter, ashtray and vape, including the emergency packet. Emergencies have a way of arriving within a week.
  • Write a small quit plan card: your date, your method, your quit aid, your supporter's number and your top reason.

Get free help to quit smoking

Talking to a doctor (GP), nurse, pharmacist or local stop smoking service is one of the most useful things you can do. They can help you choose a quit aid and the right strength.

Free quitlines:

  • US: 1-800-QUIT-NOW (1-800-784-8669)
  • UK: free local NHS stop smoking services (ask your GP or pharmacist), with similar free services in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
  • Canada: 1-866-366-3667, which links to provincial services
  • Australia: Quitline 13 7848
  • New Zealand: Quitline 0800 778 778

Line up one personal supporter as well, someone you can text when a craving hits who will cheer you on without nagging.

Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before you start. This guide is general information, not medical advice. Smoking speeds up how your body breaks down some medicines, such as certain mental health medicines, some asthma medicines and blood thinners, so tell your doctor or pharmacist you are quitting in case a dose needs checking. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have heart problems or another health condition, ask a midwife, doctor or pharmacist before using any quit aid.

How to use nicotine replacement properly

Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) gives you a measured dose of nicotine without the smoke. It takes the edge off withdrawal and cravings so you can focus on breaking the habit.

There are two groups: patches, which give a steady background dose all day, and faster forms you use when you need them, such as gum, lozenges, mouth spray, an inhalator and nasal spray. Using a patch plus a faster form together is often more effective than one type alone. The patch stops you starting each day in deep withdrawal, and the fast form covers the spikes, such as the first coffee or a stressful call.

The two big mistakes

  • Using too little. Your dose should roughly match how much you smoked. If you light up within half an hour of waking, you often need a higher dose.
  • Stopping too soon. A full course is usually around 8 to 12 weeks, then you step down gradually.

Technique matters

  • Gum: chew slowly until you feel a peppery tingle, then park it between cheek and gum. Chew again when the taste fades, park somewhere new, and repeat for about 30 minutes.
  • Mouth spray: aim at the inside of your cheek or under your tongue, and do not inhale while spraying.
  • Patch: put it on clean, dry, hairless skin and use a new spot each day. If an overnight patch gives you vivid dreams, take it off at bedtime.

Feeling mildly sick or dizzy may mean too much nicotine: cut back on the fast form and ask your pharmacist. Keep all NRT, used and unused, away from children and pets.

Prescription quit medicines and vaping to quit

Prescription quit medicines your doctor can discuss are usually started a week or two before your quit date, so ask early. They are not right for everyone: talk through side effects, mood changes and your other health conditions, and never take someone else's. Availability and cost vary by country.

For adults who smoke, switching completely to vaping is much less harmful than smoking, but it is not harm-free and the long-term effects are not fully known. Guidance and buying rules differ between countries (in Australia, for example, nicotine vapes are prescription only), so check your local rules. If you choose this route:

  • Switch completely. Smoking and vaping together does not give the same benefit.
  • Buy only from legal, regulated sellers and never modify a device.
  • Keep e-liquids away from children and pets, as nicotine liquid is poisonous if swallowed.
  • Plan to stop vaping later too, by stepping down the nicotine strength and then stopping.

If you have never smoked, do not start vaping.

Your quit day plan and the first 72 hours

The first three days are often the toughest. Nicotine leaves your system over a day or two, and you meet every daily trigger for the first time without a cigarette.

WhenWhat to do
On wakingPut on your patch or take your quit aid, drink water, read your reasons
First coffee or teaChange the mug, the room or the drink, and keep your hands busy
BreaksGo somewhere new, take a short walk, text your supporter
After mealsGet up straight away, brush your teeth or wash up
DrivingKeep gum and a fast NRT within reach, put music on, open the window

Pack a small first-week survival kit: your quit aid, a water bottle, sugar-free gum and mints, crunchy snacks such as carrot sticks or apple slices, something for your hands like a stress ball, your quitline and supporter's numbers, and a small treat for day 1, day 3 and day 7.

Days two and three can feel harder than day one. That is normal.

How to beat nicotine cravings

A single craving usually builds to a peak and fades within a few minutes, even if you do not smoke. Every time you ride one out, your brain learns that the urge passes on its own, and over the weeks cravings tend to come less often and feel weaker.

The 4 Ds

  1. Delay: tell yourself you will wait five minutes, and set a timer.
  2. Deep breathe: in through your nose for four, out slowly through your mouth for six, five or six times.
  3. Drink water: sip a cold glass slowly.
  4. Do something else: move rooms, step outside, wash up, text someone.

You can also try urge surfing. Notice the craving, find where you feel it in your body, and breathe while you watch it rise, peak and ease.

Plans for common triggers

  • Coffee: drink it somewhere different, standing up, or switch to tea for a couple of weeks.
  • Alcohol: one of the most common reasons people slip. Cut down or avoid it in the first weeks, alternate with water, and stay away from the smoking area.
  • After meals: start a new finishing ritual, such as a mint, a cup of tea or a short walk.

Nicotine withdrawal symptoms and how long they last

Common withdrawal symptoms include cravings, irritability, restlessness, low mood or anxiety, trouble concentrating, sleep changes and a bigger appetite. A cough can get a little worse at first as your lungs start to clear. These are usually strongest in the first few days to first week and mostly ease over about 2 to 4 weeks. Occasional cravings can pop up for months, usually tied to a situation, but they get weaker and rarer.

  • Sleep and jitters: smoking speeds up how fast your body clears caffeine, so the same coffee can feel much stronger after you quit. Cut back a little, especially after lunch.
  • Appetite: some people gain a little weight, which is far less harmful than smoking. Regular meals, crunchy snacks, water and a daily walk help, with no need for a diet.
When to get help. See a doctor promptly for a cough lasting more than 3 weeks, coughing up blood, breathlessness that is getting worse, unexplained weight loss or hoarseness that will not go away. Call emergency services (911 US and Canada, 999 UK, 000 Australia, 111 New Zealand) for chest pain, signs of a stroke or severe breathlessness. If you feel very low or have thoughts of harming yourself, talk to a doctor right away or call a crisis line: 988 in the US and Canada, Samaritans 116 123 in the UK, Lifeline 13 11 14 in Australia, or call or text 1737 in New Zealand.

What to do if you slip and smoke

A slip is a cigarette or a few puffs after you have quit. A relapse is going back to smoking regularly. A slip does not have to become a relapse, and it does not wipe out your smoke-free days. Many people who quit for good slipped at least once along the way.

Watch for the thought that causes most slips: just one will not hurt. A simple rule, not a single puff, leaves nothing to negotiate.

If you do slip, use the next hour well:

  1. Put it out. You do not have to finish it.
  2. Throw away the rest of the packet or the vape now, not tomorrow.
  3. Leave the situation and take a few slow breaths.
  4. Talk to yourself as you would to a friend: that was a slip, not a relapse.
  5. Keep using your quit aid as planned.
  6. Text your supporter or call your quitline.

High-risk times include weeks 1 to 4, stressful events, holidays and drinking. The book Quit Smoking and Vaping Made Simple includes a full slip plan and a 4-week quit planner if you want to map this out in detail.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most effective way to quit smoking?

Using a quit aid together with support gives you the best chance, compared with willpower alone. That usually means combination nicotine replacement (a patch plus a faster form such as gum or spray) or a prescription quit medicine, along with help from a pharmacist, doctor, quitline or stop smoking service. Add a set quit date, a cleared-out home and a plan for your main triggers.

How long do nicotine cravings last?

A single craving usually peaks and passes within a few minutes, even though it can feel much longer. Withdrawal symptoms, including frequent cravings, are usually strongest in the first few days to first week and mostly ease over about 2 to 4 weeks. Occasional cravings can return for months, usually triggered by a situation such as a drink or stress, but they become weaker and rarer.

What happens to your body when you quit smoking?

Within a day or two, carbon monoxide leaves your body and your oxygen levels improve. Within days, taste and smell start to improve. Over weeks to months, breathing and circulation get better and coughing often eases, though a cough can get a little worse at first as your lungs clear. Over the years, your risk of heart disease, stroke and many cancers keeps falling.

Will I gain weight if I quit smoking?

Some people gain a little weight after quitting, partly because appetite increases and food tastes better. A small gain is far less harmful than continuing to smoke. You do not need a strict diet: regular meals, healthy crunchy snacks for hand-to-mouth moments, plenty of water and some daily activity help. Focus on staying smoke-free first.

Is vaping a good way to quit smoking?

For adults who smoke, switching completely to vaping is much less harmful than smoking, though it is not harm-free and long-term effects are not fully known. It only helps if you stop smoking entirely, as using both does not give the same benefit. Rules differ between countries, so check local guidance, buy from legal sellers, and plan to step down and stop vaping later.

Your first step today: pick a quit date one to two weeks from now, write it on your calendar, and call your local quitline or talk to a pharmacist about which nicotine replacement or quit aid would suit you. Everything else in your plan can be built from there.
