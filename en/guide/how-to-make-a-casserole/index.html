How to Make a Casserole: The Simple 5-Layer Method That Always Works

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you want a hot, filling dinner that bakes in one dish while you get on with your evening, a casserole is the answer. To make a casserole, layer a starch (rice, pasta or potatoes), a cooked protein, some vegetables and a generous sauce in a 9 x 13 in (23 x 33 cm) dish, add a topping, then bake at 350 to 375°F (180 to 190°C, 160 to 170°C fan) for 30 to 45 minutes until the center is hot and the edges bubble.

That is the whole idea. This guide shows you how to build one from whatever is in your kitchen, how to make a quick sauce instead of opening a can of soup, how to avoid soggy or dry results, and how to make ahead and freeze casseroles safely.

The 5 layers of any casserole

Almost every casserole, from a chicken and rice bake to a shepherd's pie, follows the same pattern. Once you see it, you can invent your own without a recipe.

  1. The base. A starch that soaks up sauce: rice, pasta, potatoes, bread, tortillas or biscuits. Some go in cooked, some go in raw and cook in the liquid.
  2. The protein. Cooked chicken or turkey, browned ground beef (beef mince), sausage, tuna, eggs, beans or lentils.
  3. The vegetables. Onion and garlic for flavor, then broccoli, spinach, peppers, mushrooms, peas or whatever needs using up.
  4. The sauce. Cream sauce, cheese sauce, tomato sauce or broth (stock). This holds everything together and keeps it moist.
  5. The topping. Shredded (grated) cheese, buttery breadcrumbs, crushed crackers, mashed potato, biscuits or cornbread batter.

Season each layer lightly as you go. A casserole salted only at the end tastes flat in the middle, and you cannot stir salt into a finished bake without wrecking the topping.

Which casserole dish size to use

You only need two or three dishes to cook almost any casserole.

DishHolds aboutServes
9 x 13 in (23 x 33 cm)3 quarts (3 liters)6 to 8
8 x 8 in (20 x 20 cm)2 quarts (2 liters)3 to 4, or sides
Round or oval casserole dish2 to 3 quarts (2 to 3 liters)4 to 6

To halve a 9 x 13 in recipe, use an 8 x 8 in dish. The depth stays about the same, so the time drops only a little. Start checking 10 minutes early.

The material changes things a bit too. Glass and ceramic heat slowly and keep food warm at the table. Metal heats faster, browns the edges more and is the safest choice if you ever want to bake straight from the freezer. A deeper dish needs longer for the middle to get hot, while a shallow one cooks faster and gives you more crispy top.

How to make a casserole without canned soup

Many classic recipes start with a can of condensed cream soup. It works, but a basic white sauce takes about 10 minutes, costs very little and lets you control the salt. This amount makes about 2 1/2 cups (600 ml), enough to replace two 10.5 oz (298 g) cans.

  • 4 tablespoons (55 g) butter
  • 1/3 cup (40 g) all-purpose flour (plain flour)
  • 1 cup (240 ml) chicken or vegetable broth (stock)
  • 1 1/4 cups (300 ml) whole milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt and 1/4 teaspoon black pepper

Melt the butter over medium heat, stir in the flour and cook for 1 to 2 minutes. Whisk in the broth, then the milk, a splash at a time so no lumps form. Simmer for 3 to 4 minutes, whisking, until it coats a spoon, then season.

Easy variations

  • Cream of mushroom: cook 8 oz (225 g) chopped mushrooms in the butter before adding the flour.
  • Cream of chicken: use chicken broth and add a pinch of dried thyme.
  • Cheese sauce: stir in 1 cup (100 g) shredded cheddar off the heat.
  • Gluten-free: thicken with 3 tablespoons cornstarch (cornflour) whisked into the cold milk instead of the flour, and check broth labels.

Oven temperature and how long to bake a casserole

Most casseroles bake at one of three settings. Ovens vary, so use the times as a guide and check early.

SettingGood for
350°F (180°C, 160°C fan)Deep bakes, egg casseroles, reheating
375°F (190°C, 170°C fan)Most chicken, rice and pasta bakes
400°F (200°C, 180°C fan)Shallow bakes and crisp potato toppings

A typical 9 x 13 in casserole made with cooked ingredients takes 30 to 45 minutes. A good rhythm is to bake it covered with foil for the first 20 minutes or so, which traps moisture, then uncover it for the last 15 to 20 minutes so the top browns and crisps.

It is done when the edges bubble and the center is hot. For any casserole containing meat, poultry or eggs, push an instant-read thermometer into the middle of the dish, not near an edge, and look for 165°F (74°C).

Then let it rest for 10 minutes. Cut it straight away and the sauce runs everywhere and the portions slump. After a short rest, the sauce thickens and every serving holds its shape.

How to keep a casserole from getting soggy or dry

Almost every casserole disappointment comes down to moisture. Too much and the bottom turns to mush. Too little and the pasta goes chalky and the edges turn leathery.

To avoid a soggy casserole

  • Undercook pasta by 2 to 3 minutes. It keeps soaking up sauce in the oven.
  • Squeeze cooked or thawed spinach hard in a clean towel.
  • Cook watery vegetables such as mushrooms, zucchini (courgette) and eggplant (aubergine) first, so their liquid steams off in the pan.
  • Drain browned ground meat well.
  • Use cold, day-old rice rather than freshly cooked rice.
  • Bake uncovered for the last part so steam can escape.

To avoid a dry casserole

  • Use enough sauce to coat everything generously. It should look slightly too saucy going in.
  • If you use uncooked rice or pasta, follow the liquid amounts exactly.
  • Keep it covered for most of the baking time.
  • Stop baking once the center is hot. Extra time only dries it out.

Casserole toppings that stay crisp

The topping is the part people argue over, so give it a moment's thought.

  • Cheese melts and browns best when added for the last 15 to 20 minutes, uncovered. Shred it from a block if you can, since pre-shredded cheese has a coating that can stop it melting smoothly.
  • For classic crunch, toss 1 cup (60 g) panko or fresh breadcrumbs with 2 tablespoons (30 g) melted butter before scattering it on. Crushed crackers, tortilla chips and crispy fried onions work the same way.
  • Mashed potato, biscuits and cornbread batter turn a casserole into a pie. Spread them over a hot filling when you can, so the underside starts cooking straight away.

If the top browns before the middle is hot, lay a loose sheet of foil over the dish and keep going.

Two easy ideas to try

A chicken and broccoli rice bake mixes cooked rice, shredded chicken and broccoli florets into a cheesy white sauce, with a panko and cheddar topping, baked at 375°F (190°C, 170°C fan). A cottage pie (beef) or shepherd's pie (lamb) layers a savory ground meat and vegetable filling under mashed potato and bakes until the peaks turn golden.

How to make a casserole ahead of time

Casseroles are made for planning ahead. To prepare one for the next day:

  1. Cool any hot cooked ingredients first, then assemble the casserole.
  2. Cover it tightly and refrigerate for up to 24 hours.
  3. Leave crunchy toppings off until just before baking so they stay crisp.
  4. Take the dish out 20 to 30 minutes before baking to take the chill off.
  5. Add 10 to 20 minutes to the baking time, since it starts cold, and check the center with a thermometer.

Never layer raw ground meat into a casserole to cook in the oven. Brown it first until it reaches 160°F (71°C), drain it and then assemble.

Can you freeze a casserole before baking

Yes, many casseroles freeze very well, some before baking and some after. Dishes with cooked pasta, rice, meat and sturdy sauces freeze well. Raw potatoes, crisp toppings and dishes heavy on mayonnaise or sour cream usually do not.

Casserole typeFreeze
Chicken or turkey in cream sauceYes, before baking
Beef or pork in tomato sauceYes, before or after baking
Pasta bakesYes, before baking
Rice and grain bakesYes, after baking
Potato gratinsUsually no
Egg and breakfast bakesYes, after baking

Press plastic wrap (cling film) onto the surface, cover the dish tightly with foil and label it with the name, date, oven temperature and time. Freeze for up to 3 months. A handy trick is to line the dish with foil, freeze the casserole, then lift out the frozen block and wrap it, so your dish is free again.

Thaw overnight in the refrigerator, or up to 24 hours for a deep 9 x 13 in dish, never on the counter. Bake as usual, adding 10 to 20 minutes and keeping it covered for most of the time. To reheat a baked casserole, cover it and warm it at 350°F (180°C, 160°C fan) until the center reaches 165°F (74°C), about 30 to 45 minutes for a full dish.

Safety note. Glass and ceramic dishes can crack or shatter if they go from the freezer straight into a hot oven, or from a hot oven onto a cold, wet surface. Only bake from frozen in metal or clearly freezer-to-oven rated dishes. Cool leftovers and refrigerate them within 2 hours, and eat them within 3 to 4 days.

Taking a casserole to a potluck

A casserole is the easiest dish to bring along, as long as it arrives hot and in one piece.

  • Time the bake so it comes out just before you leave.
  • Cover it with a lid or a double layer of foil, wrap it in a thick towel and set it in an insulated bag or sturdy box.
  • Put a nonslip mat or folded towel in the car so the dish cannot slide.
  • Pack a serving spoon, a trivet and a label with the dish name and main allergens.
  • Serve hot food within 2 hours, then refrigerate or discard what is left.

Stick a strip of masking tape with your name on the bottom of the dish. Casserole dishes have a way of going home with the wrong people. Frequently asked questions

What temperature do you bake a casserole at?

Most casseroles bake at 350 to 375°F (180 to 190°C, 160 to 170°C fan). Use 350°F for deep dishes, egg bakes and reheating, 375°F for most chicken, rice and pasta bakes, and 400°F (200°C, 180°C fan) for shallow dishes or crisp potato toppings. Ovens vary, so check early and confirm the center is hot.

Should a casserole be covered when baking?

Usually yes, for most of the time. Cover the dish with foil for the first 20 minutes or so to trap moisture and help the center heat through, then uncover it for the last 15 to 20 minutes so the topping can brown and crisp and extra steam can escape. If the top browns too early, lay foil loosely back over it.

How do you know when a casserole is done?

The edges should bubble and the center should be steaming hot. For any casserole containing meat, poultry or eggs, the most reliable check is an instant-read thermometer pushed into the middle of the dish. It should read 165°F (74°C). Then rest the casserole for 10 minutes before cutting so the sauce can settle.

Can I put a cold casserole straight in the oven?

Yes, a casserole you assembled ahead and refrigerated can go into a heated oven, but add 10 to 20 minutes to the baking time. Taking it out 20 to 30 minutes beforehand helps, especially with glass or ceramic. Never put a glass or ceramic dish straight from the freezer into a hot oven, because it can crack.

Can you put raw meat in a casserole?

Do not layer raw ground meat into a casserole to cook in the oven. Brown it first until it reaches 160°F (71°C) and drain it. Some recipes do bake raw chicken pieces in the sauce, which works when the chicken is in a single layer and the center of the dish reaches 165°F (74°C). When in doubt, use cooked meat.

How long does a casserole last in the fridge?

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of baking, in shallow containers so they chill quickly, and eat them within 3 to 4 days. Reheat portions covered until they reach 165°F (74°C). A splash of milk or broth over pasta and rice bakes before reheating stops them drying out. For longer storage, freeze portions for up to 3 months.

Tonight, look in your refrigerator and pick one item for each of the five layers: a starch, a protein, a vegetable, a sauce and a topping. Make the 10 minute white sauce, assemble everything in your 9 x 13 in dish, bake it covered and then uncovered, and check the middle for 165°F (74°C). Once you have done it once, you will never need a recipe to get a good casserole on the table.
