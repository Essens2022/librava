Keto Diet for Beginners: How to Start Safely and Simply

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you have decided to try keto but feel lost among macros, test strips and conflicting advice, start here. The short answer: keto means eating very little carbohydrate (many beginners start at around 20 to 30 g of net carbs a day), moderate protein and more fat, with plenty of non-starchy vegetables, eggs, fish, poultry, olive oil, avocado, nuts and cheese, and almost no bread, pasta, rice, potatoes, sugar or most fruit.

Below you will find how keto works in plain words, how to read net carbs on labels in different countries, what to eat and what to limit, simple swaps, how to ease in and handle keto flu, who should talk to a doctor first, a sample day and the honest pros and cons.

What is the keto diet, simply explained

A ketogenic diet is very low in carbohydrate, moderate in protein and higher in fat. With very little carbohydrate coming in, your body uses more fat for fuel, and your liver makes substances called ketones. That state is called nutritional ketosis.

Nutritional ketosis is not the same as ketoacidosis. Ketoacidosis is a dangerous condition, mainly in people with type 1 diabetes and some people on certain diabetes medicines, where ketones and blood acid levels rise out of control. That difference is one reason some people must talk to a doctor before starting.

Testing ketones is optional. Urine strips become less reliable over time, and fewer ketones later on is normal, not a sign of failure. If you have diabetes, follow your doctor's advice on testing. Medical keto diets, such as those used for some types of epilepsy, are planned by specialist teams and are different from everyday keto.

Who should not start keto without a doctor

Keto changes how your body handles fluids, salt and blood sugar, and it can change how some medicines work. Talk to your doctor first, and only go ahead with their approval and supervision, if you have:

  • Diabetes treated with medicines, especially insulin or sulfonylureas (blood sugar can drop too low) or SGLT2 inhibitors, whose names usually end in gliflozin (keto can raise the risk of ketoacidosis even with normal blood sugar).
  • Type 1 diabetes.
  • Kidney disease or a history of kidney stones, liver disease, a history of pancreatitis, or gallbladder disease or removal.
  • Heart disease or high cholesterol.
  • Blood pressure medicines or diuretics (water tablets), because blood pressure can drop.
  • Any long-term condition or regular medicine, or older age with low weight or frailty.

Keto is not recommended if you are pregnant, trying to get pregnant or breastfeeding, if you have a current or past eating disorder, or for children and teenagers unless a specialist runs it.

Never stop, skip or change a medicine because you have started keto. Your doctor may need to adjust doses before your first day, so have that conversation first, and ask about a cholesterol test before you start and again a few months in.

Net carbs and how to read labels

Net carbs are total carbohydrate minus fiber. The tricky part is that label rules differ by country.

Label fromCarbohydrate figureWhat you do
US and CanadaIncludes fiberSubtract fiber to get net carbs
UK and EuropeAlready excludes fiberUse it as it is
Australia and New ZealandAlready excludes fiberUse it as it is

So a US label showing 10 g total carbohydrate and 4 g fiber means about 6 g net carbs. Subtracting fiber again from a UK or Australian label is a common mistake that makes food look lower in carbs than it is.

Some products also subtract sugar alcohols, which affect people differently and can upset your stomach. Be wary of anything marketed as keto or low net carb. Whole foods are a far better base.

How many carbs a day on keto

Many people start at around 20 to 30 g of net carbs a day. Typical keto ranges run from about 20 to 50 g, and the right range varies with body size, activity and health. Above that, roughly 50 to 130 g, is usually called low carb or moderate low carb, which is a perfectly reasonable way to eat for many people.

You do not need to hit an exact number on day one. A simple way to plan is by rough bands per meal:

  • Two meals at about 5 to 10 g net carbs each.
  • One meal at about 5 g or less.
  • A small snack, such as a handful of almonds or cucumber with cream cheese.

That lands most people comfortably inside a typical starting range. Treat every figure as approximate, since the size of an onion or the brand of a sauce changes the real number.

Keto food list for beginners

Picture a keto plate as half non-starchy vegetables, a palm-sized portion of protein and a generous splash of healthy fat.

Foods to eat

  • Non-starchy vegetables: leafy greens, zucchini (courgette), cauliflower, broccoli, cabbage, green beans, mushrooms, asparagus, cucumber, eggplant (aubergine) and bell peppers.
  • Protein: eggs, fish and seafood, chicken, turkey and tofu, with beef, pork and lamb in moderation.
  • Healthy fats: olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds and oily fish, eaten about twice a week.
  • Dairy: cheese, small amounts of plain full-fat yogurt, some cream and butter.
  • Flavor: herbs, spices, garlic, lemon, vinegar and mustard.

Foods to limit

  • Bread, pasta, rice, oats, cereal and other grains.
  • Potatoes, sweet potatoes, corn and parsnips.
  • Beans, lentils and chickpeas.
  • Most fruit, apart from small portions of berries.
  • Sugar, honey, syrups, juice, regular soda and sweet sauces.

Keto is not bacon and cheese all day. Processed meats fit the carb rules but are best kept occasional, and saturated fats such as butter, cream and coconut oil are best kept moderate, with mostly unsaturated fats doing the heavy lifting.

Easy keto swaps for everyday meals

Most familiar meals become keto with one or two swaps.

Mashed potatoesCreamy cauliflower mash

Lasagna sheetsThin zucchini or eggplant slices
Tortillas or burger bunsLarge lettuce leaves or a bowl
CroutonsToasted seeds or baked Parmesan crisps
Chips (crisps)Roasted almonds
Sweet bottled dressingsOlive oil, lemon and herbs

If your family is not on keto, cook one main dish and add a carb on the side for them: crispy chicken thighs with green beans for you and roast potatoes for them, or a curry over cauliflower rice for you and regular rice for everyone else. Keep a bag of frozen broccoli and one of frozen cauliflower rice in the freezer, and a keto side is always about 10 minutes away.

MealIdea
BreakfastTwo eggs scrambled with spinach and mushrooms, cooked in olive oil

SnackA small handful of almonds, or celery with cream cheese
DinnerRoast chicken thighs with garlic green beans and creamy cauliflower mash

Cook chicken to 165°F (74°C), egg dishes until set, and fish to 145°F (63°C) or until it flakes. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and eat them within 3 to 4 days, or cooked fish within 1 to 2 days. Keto flu and how to start keto safely

Easing in over a week or two is gentler than cutting carbs overnight. Even so, in the first days to about two weeks many people feel headaches, tiredness, brain fog, irritability, dizziness, cramps or nausea, often linked to losing water and salt. This is often called keto flu. Food-based fixes help:

  • Drink plenty of fluids, and check that your urine is pale yellow.
  • Salt your food to taste and have a mug of broth once or twice a day, unless a doctor has told you to limit salt.
  • Eat potassium- and magnesium-rich foods: leafy greens, avocado, mushrooms, nuts, seeds and fish.
  • Rest more and keep exercise gentle for the first week.

Do not take potassium or other mineral supplements without medical advice, since they can be dangerous for some people. Constipation is common too, so add fiber from vegetables, nuts, seeds and ground flaxseed, with fluids. Some people notice bad breath, poorer sleep or weaker workouts at first.

Review how you feel after a few weeks to a few months. Over the long term, studies suggest weight results are broadly similar to other eating patterns people can keep up, so it is fine to stop or move to a moderate low-carb pattern by adding back whole grains, beans, fruit and starchy vegetables gradually.

Stop and get medical advice if symptoms are severe, last more than about two weeks, or include fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat, confusion or vomiting. Get urgent help for nausea or vomiting with tummy pain, fast or deep breathing, extreme thirst, unusual sleepiness or fruity-smelling breath, especially if you have diabetes. Call 911 (US, Canada), 999 (UK), 000 (Australia) or 111 (New Zealand).

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get into ketosis?

It varies from person to person, but many people reach nutritional ketosis within a few days of keeping net carbs very low. Easing in over a week or two takes longer but is usually gentler. You do not need to test to follow keto. If you have diabetes or take medicines, talk to your doctor about whether and how to test before you start.

Can I eat fruit on keto?

Most fruit is too high in carbohydrate for keto, but small portions of berries, such as a few raspberries or strawberries, usually fit. Avocado, tomatoes and olives are technically fruit and work well too. Fruit juice, bananas, grapes and dried fruit are much higher in sugar. If you later move to a moderate low-carb pattern, fruit is one of the first foods to add back.

What can I drink on keto?

Water, sparkling water, herbal tea, tea and coffee without sugar, and broth are all good choices. Avoid regular soda, juice, sweetened coffee drinks and sugary mixers. Low-carb alcoholic drinks still contain alcohol, and your tolerance may drop on keto, so follow your country's low-risk guidance and never drink on an empty stomach if you take diabetes medicines.

Is keto safe for everyone?

No. Keto is not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding, for people with a current or past eating disorder, or for children and teenagers unless a specialist runs it. People with diabetes on medicines, kidney, liver, gallbladder or heart disease, high cholesterol, a history of pancreatitis or on blood pressure medicines need a doctor's approval and supervision first.

Do I need to count calories on keto?

Keto is mainly about keeping carbohydrate low, so most beginners focus on net carbs rather than calories. Building plates from non-starchy vegetables, a palm-sized portion of protein and a sensible amount of fat keeps portions reasonable without heavy tracking. Fat is filling, but you do not need to add butter to everything. Ask a doctor or dietitian if you have specific goals.

Does keto raise cholesterol?

In some people, LDL cholesterol rises on keto, while others see little change. That is why it is wise to have a blood cholesterol test before you start and again a few months later. Choosing mostly unsaturated fats such as olive oil, avocado, nuts and oily fish, and keeping butter, cream and processed meats moderate, is the healthier pattern.

Your first step today is not a shopping spree. Book a quick chat with your doctor if any condition or medicine applies to you, then plan three keto dinners for next week built on vegetables, a palm-sized protein and olive oil, and start easing your carbs down over the following week or two.
