Acrylic Painting for Beginners: Supplies, Mixing and a First Picture

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you have always wanted to paint but worry you are not artistic, acrylics are the friendliest place to start. The short answer: buy eight colors, a handful of synthetic brushes and some canvas panels, learn to mix from a limited palette, practice a few basic brush marks, then paint a simple sunset sky as your first picture.

This guide walks you through each of those steps in plain words, with a shopping list, a mixing chart exercise, a first painting in stages and a table for fixing the most common problems. You do not need talent, only a table corner and a willingness to make a few muddy puddles along the way.

What you need to start acrylic painting

The art aisle will try to sell you a suitcase of supplies. You need far less, and a small kit teaches you more because it forces you to mix. Here is a starter list that covers a whole first year of practice:

  • Eight tubes of paint (see the limited palette below)
  • Synthetic brushes: a large and a medium flat, a small round, a filbert, a liner and one old scruffy brush for texture
  • One plastic or metal palette knife with a bent (cranked) handle
  • Canvas panels around 8 x 10 inch (20 x 25 cm), or a pad of canvas paper for practice
  • A lidded plastic tray, paper towel and baking paper (greaseproof paper in the UK) for a stay-wet palette
  • Two water jars, a roll of paper towel (kitchen roll) and an old cloth
  • Low-tack masking tape, an old toothbrush and a small kitchen sponge

Choose synthetic brushes because acrylic is hard on natural hair. Student grade paint is perfectly fine to learn on. Artist grade has more pigment, so colors are stronger and go further, but you can upgrade a tube at a time later.

Skip big boxed sets, black paint, expensive brushes and a floor easel for now. Prop your panel against a stack of books and paint by daylight from the side.

The best acrylic colors for beginners

A limited palette of eight colors can mix almost anything you will want to paint. The trick is having a warm and a cool version of each primary.

ColorWhat it does

Cool redPink or purple-leaning red for violets and flowers
Warm yellowDeep, golden yellow

On the tube, a warm red may be labeled scarlet or light red and a cool red crimson or rose. A cool yellow is often called lemon. Notice there is no black: you will mix richer darks yourself.

How acrylic paint behaves, and why it matters

Acrylic is pigment held in an acrylic polymer emulsion. While wet it is water based, so you thin it and clean up with water. Once dry it forms a flexible, water-resistant film, which is why paint must never dry in your brushes.

Two quirks catch every beginner. First, it dries fast: thin layers are often touch dry in 10 to 30 minutes. Second, it usually dries slightly darker than it looks wet. When you mix a color to match something, make it a touch lighter than you think you need.

Make a stay-wet palette in five minutes

  1. Lay two or three sheets of damp paper towel in a shallow lidded tray.
  2. Smooth a sheet of baking paper on top.
  3. Squeeze your paints onto the paper.
  4. Put the lid on whenever you take a break.

Paint stays workable for hours, often days. Throw out the towels if mold appears.

How to mix colors with acrylic paint

Picture a clock face with red at the top, yellow at four o'clock and blue at eight. Orange, green and purple sit in the gaps. Colors opposite each other are complements: red and green, blue and orange, yellow and purple. Side by side they make each other look brighter. Mixed together they dull each other toward brown or gray.

For a bright secondary, mix the two primaries that lean toward each other. Warm red plus warm yellow gives a glowing orange. Cool yellow plus phthalo blue gives a vivid green. Cool red plus ultramarine gives a rich violet. Mix the ones that lean away and you get muted versions, which often look more like nature.

  • Tint: add white (red becomes pink)
  • Shade: add a little burnt umber or the complement instead of black
  • Tone: add a gray made from white and a dark

Always start with the lighter color and add the darker one bit by bit, using the palette knife. Phthalo blue is so strong that a speck on the tip of the knife can turn a whole pile of yellow green.

Natural greens and soft darks

Bright mixed greens can look like a highlighter pen. Add a speck of warm red or burnt umber to calm them, because red is green's complement. For a deep near-black, mix ultramarine and burnt umber in roughly equal amounts. Push it toward blue for night skies or toward brown for warm shadows, and add white for a whole family of grays.

Easy acrylic brush techniques to practice first

Every painting is built from a small set of marks. Learn them on scrap paper and your first real pictures will feel familiar. Load only the front third or half of the bristles, and aim for paint about as thick as soft butter. A useful rule is no more water than paint.

  • Flat strokes side by side with a large flat brush, for skies and backgrounds
  • Wet into wet blending, laying two bands of color and brushing lightly across the join while both are wet
  • Dry brush, wiping most of the paint off and skimming the brush low over the surface for foam, bark and grass
  • Stippling, tapping the brush straight down for foliage and gravel
  • Glazing, a thin see-through layer over dry paint to deepen a shadow or add a glow
  • Scumbling, a broken lighter layer scrubbed over a dark one for mist and soft cloud edges
  • Palette knife drags and quick press and lift marks for petals
  • Masking tape edges for crisp straight lines

Acrylic blending needs speed. If the paint starts to drag, stop, let it dry and blend a second thin layer on top. Two quick layers usually look smoother than one long struggle. A slow-drying medium (retarder) or a light mist of water also buys you time.

Spend 20 minutes warming up on scrap paper before each session. Paint stripes, blends and dots in two or three colors. It loosens your hand and takes the fear out of a clean white canvas.

Your first acrylic painting, a sunset sky step by step

Skies make the best first painting. They are mostly blending, they forgive wobbly edges and they need no drawing. Use an 8 x 10 inch (20 x 25 cm) panel held landscape, and allow about an hour plus drying time.

  1. Draw a gentle, uneven hill line about a quarter of the way up. Make one side lower than the other.
  2. Mix five generous piles: pale yellow, warm yellow, orange, a pinkish red and a soft purple. Running out halfway is the top reason skies go patchy.
  3. With the large flat brush, paint pale yellow just above the hill line, carrying it slightly below. Add warm yellow above and blend the join with light side to side strokes.
  4. Work upward through orange, red and finally purple, blending each join and wiping the brush between colors.
  5. Let it dry for 15 to 20 minutes, then look from across the room. If it looks streaky, repeat with a thinner second layer.
  6. Mix ultramarine and burnt umber into a near-black and paint the hill as a flat, solid shape, with a slightly bumpy top edge for distant bushes.
  7. Dab a small pale sun just above the hill and soften its edge with a dry brush.

Keep the bands in order. Yellow and purple are complements, so blending them directly turns the middle of your sky brown.

Common acrylic painting mistakes and how to fix them

Because acrylic dries fast and covers well, a painting that goes wrong is simply a painting that is not finished yet. Let the area dry and paint over it. Light over dark may need two or three coats.

ProblemLikely causeFix
Muddy, dull colorToo many colors in one mixLet dry, repaint with a fresh mix
Streaky or hard blendPaint set while blendingGlaze or scumble over the line
Painting looks flatEverything is a middle valueDeepen darks, add a few bright lights
Colors look heavier dryNormal color shiftMix a little lighter
Paint lifts or rubs offToo much waterUse less water or a glazing medium
Busy, cluttered pictureDetail everywhereKeep detail only at the focal point

To avoid mud, use no more than three colors in a mix plus white, rinse and wipe your brush between color families and change rinse water when it looks like strong tea. When you cannot name the one change that would help most, stop.

Safety and cleanup for painting at home

Many acrylics carry a non-toxic label, but some pigments, such as cadmium and cobalt colors, are hazardous if swallowed or breathed in as dust.

Never eat, drink or smoke while painting, and never point a brush with your lips. Keep paint away from young children and pets and wash your hands afterward. Wear a dust mask if you sand dry paint, never heat or burn dried paint, and use spray varnish outdoors or with good ventilation. If paint gets in your eyes, rinse with plenty of water and seek medical advice if irritation continues.
  1. Wipe excess paint off the brush onto paper towel.
  2. Rinse in the murky jar, then the clean jar.
  3. Wash gently with soap and lukewarm water near the metal ferrule.
  4. Reshape the bristles and dry the brush flat or upright, never on its tip.

Do not pour dirty rinse water down the sink each time. Let it settle in a jar for a day or two, pour off the clear water, let the sludge dry and put it in household waste, following local rules.

How to varnish and keep improving

Touch dry is not fully dry. Wait at least 24 to 72 hours before varnishing a normal painting, and a week or more for thick knife work, then follow your varnish label. Sign before you varnish so your signature sits under the protective layer. Apply thin, even coats with a soft wide brush on a flat painting, and cover it loosely to keep dust off. Satin suits most beginner work.

To keep improving, paint one small picture a week, work from your own photos and date your warm-up sheets so you can see your progress.

Frequently asked questions

Is acrylic painting good for beginners?

Yes. Acrylic is one of the easiest paints to start with because it is water based while wet, cleans up with soap and water and dries quickly. That speed lets you layer and fix mistakes in a single evening by painting over them. The main thing to learn is that it dries fast and slightly darker, so you work in quick sections and mix colors a touch lighter.

What should I paint first with acrylics?

A simple sunset sky over a dark hill silhouette is an ideal first painting. It is mostly blending, needs almost no drawing and forgives wobbly edges. Other easy starters are soft clouds over a field, two lemons on a cloth or a full moon over pine tree shapes. Choose subjects with big simple shapes before trying detailed scenes.

Do I need to add water to acrylic paint?

A little, often. Heavy body paint straight from the tube can be stiff, so dampen your brush or add a drop of water until it feels like soft butter or thick cream. A good rule is no more water than paint. Too much water weakens the binder, so the paint can turn patchy, lift or rub off. For very thin glazes, a glazing medium is safer than lots of water.

How do you keep acrylic paint from drying out on the palette?

Use a stay-wet palette. Put damp paper towel in the bottom of a lidded plastic tray, lay baking paper (greaseproof paper) on top and squeeze your paint onto that. Close the lid during breaks. Moisture seeps up through the paper and keeps paint workable for hours, often days. A light mist of water from a spray bottle also helps during a session.

Can you paint acrylic on regular paper?

Thin printer paper buckles and soaks up paint, so it is not a good choice. Use canvas paper or heavy mixed media paper of about 140 lb (300 gsm) or heavier for practice, and canvas panels or stretched canvas for paintings you want to keep. Most store canvases are already primed with gesso. Raw wood or bare card needs a coat or two of gesso first.

How do you blend acrylic paint smoothly?

Work fast and in bands. Paint one color, immediately paint the next beside it with a slight overlap, then brush lightly back and forth across the join while both are wet. Wipe the brush and repeat with a lighter touch. If the paint drags, stop, let it dry and blend a thin second layer on top. A slow-drying medium gives you more working time.

Your first step today is small: gather the eight colors and a few brushes, make a stay-wet palette from a lidded tray, and spend 20 minutes painting stripes and blends on scrap paper.
