How to Start a Lawn Care Business: A Practical First-Season Plan

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you already mow a few lawns for neighbors and enjoy working outside, you may be wondering how to turn that into a proper business. To start a lawn care business, offer mowing and basic garden maintenance first, check local rules and register the business, get liability insurance, price every visit from your real costs and time, and fill one tight round of streets before spreading out.

This guide walks you through those steps in order, with an equipment list, a worked pricing example and a seasonal plan. It also explains the safety and chemical rules that catch many new lawn businesses out.

Is a lawn care business right for you

The good parts are easy to see: fresh air, visible results, regular customers and work you can start with modest equipment. The hard parts matter just as much. The work is physical, the weather decides your week, and the busy growing season is followed by quiet months that you must plan for.

Rate yourself honestly

  • Can you handle a full day of pushing, lifting and loading in heat or drizzle?
  • Are you comfortable maintaining small engines or battery tools?
  • Will you turn up on schedule, every week, for the same customers?
  • Can you keep simple records, send invoices and chase payments politely?

Many people start on weekends as a side business, then go full time once their round is full. Expect to build slowly. Talk it through at home too, since early starts, a noisy trailer and the occasional rained-off Saturday affect the whole household.

Choose your lawn and garden services

Start with a clear menu. Regular mowing and edging are the bread and butter of most lawn businesses, and extras are where much of the skill goes.

  • Core mowing and edging
  • Trimming and strimming around beds, fences and paths
  • Hedge trimming from the ground or a stable platform
  • Weeding and bed maintenance
  • Leaf cleanup in fall (autumn)
  • Spring and fall cleanups
  • Green waste removal to licensed facilities
  • Overseeding and aeration once you have experience

What to leave to specialists

Tree work and chainsaw use need proper training and certification. Irrigation installation is specialist work. Chemical treatments such as weed killers and feed and weed products usually need a license or certificate when you apply them for pay, so many new businesses refer them on, as a later section explains.

Legal basics, licenses and local rules

Rules vary by country, state, province and council, so treat this as a list of questions to answer locally. It is general information, not legal or tax advice.

  1. Draw your service area on a map and choose a business name.
  2. Register the business as your country requires.
  3. Ask about local business licenses, vehicle and trailer rules and parking.
  4. Check noise rules, since many areas limit the hours you can run loud power tools.
  5. Find licensed green waste facilities. In the UK, carrying other people's waste usually needs a waste carrier registration; elsewhere, check local rules.
  6. Set up records from day one and ask an accountant how much to set aside for taxes.

Write down what you learn, who told you and when. That one page saves hours of searching when you renew or expand.

Insurance before your first lawn

A stone thrown by a mower or string trimmer (strimmer) can crack a window or a car door in a second. Liability insurance, called public liability in the UK, Australia and New Zealand, should be in place before your first paid job.

CoverWhat it protects
Liability or public liabilityDamage to property and injury to others
Equipment and tools coverTheft or damage to mowers and tools
Vehicle insurance for business useYour truck, van or ute and trailer at work
Employer's coverRequired once you hire, rules vary by country

In the UK, employer's liability insurance is a legal requirement once you employ staff. Workers' compensation rules apply to employees in the US, Canada and Australia, and New Zealand has an accident compensation levy. Use written service agreements, take before and after photos, and report any damage to the customer honestly and promptly.

Choose between gas (petrol) and battery tools based on your customers and local noise rules; battery tools suit noise-sensitive areas and early starts. Lock tools in the vehicle and never leave them on view. Keep a maintenance log, service equipment in the quiet season and keep blades sharp, since a dull blade tears grass and leaves brown tips customers notice.

Mower safety and working safely

Safety and the label always come first. Before every job, walk the lawn and clear stones, toys, sticks and debris, and look for hidden hazards like sprinkler heads, cables and wasp nests.

  • Protective gear. Eye and hearing protection, sturdy closed-toe boots (steel or composite toe recommended), long trousers and gloves.
  • Keep people and pets well away, especially children
  • Stop the engine, wait for the blade to stop and disconnect the spark plug or remove the battery before clearing clogs or adjusting anything
  • Mow across slopes with walk-behind mowers; follow the manufacturer for ride-on mowers, which usually go up and down
  • Avoid steep or wet slopes and drop-offs, and never carry passengers
  • Refuel only outdoors, with the engine off and cool, using approved fuel containers
  • Watch where trimmers and blowers throw debris near cars, windows and people
Outdoor work brings health risks too. Wear sun protection, drink plenty of water, take breaks in shade and start early in hot weather. Learn the signs of heat illness. Anyone with a known severe allergy to stings should carry their prescribed medication, and for a severe reaction call emergency services (911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK, 000 in Australia, 111 in New Zealand). Ask your doctor whether your tetanus protection is up to date.

Pesticide licenses and fertilizers

Applying weed killers, insecticides, fungicides or feed and weed products for pay usually requires a license, certification or certificate of competence. Examples include state or provincial applicator licensing in the US and Canada, certificates of competence in the UK and state or territory licensing in Australia. Some areas also restrict or ban cosmetic pesticide use on lawns.

Many new businesses start chemical-free, focusing on mowing and maintenance, and refer treatments to licensed operators. Some even market organic or chemical-free care as their niche.

If you do become licensed, follow the label exactly; in the US the label is legally enforceable. Wear the protective equipment the label requires, store products locked in original containers, never spray before rain or in wind, keep children and pets off treated areas, protect ponds and drains, and never spray flowering plants where bees feed.

How much to charge for mowing a lawn

Work out what each billable hour must bring in, then price visits from the time they really take. Add up what you want to pay yourself, your yearly running costs (fuel, servicing, blades, insurance, vehicle, disposal fees, phone, saving to replace equipment) and money set aside for taxes. Divide by the hours you can actually bill once travel, loading, quoting and rain days are counted.

Example only, with made-up round numbers: pay 30,000, costs 10,000 and taxes 8,000 make 48,000. Divided by 1,200 billable hours, each hour must bring in 40.

Use a minimum charge so tiny lawns still cover the drive and unloading. Quote from a quick site visit, in writing, stating what is and is not included. Seasonal contracts spread payments evenly through the year, which helps your cash flow in quiet months. Research local rates, but never try to be the cheapest.

Finding customers and planning the year

Start with neighbors, friends and family, then target nearby streets so your round stays tight. Ten lawns on three streets mean far less unpaid driving than ten lawns spread across town.

  • Drop flyers or door hangers on the streets around existing customers, where allowed
  • Add your name and number to your vehicle
  • Set up your free local map listing and a simple website
  • Use neighborhood apps and home services marketplaces carefully
  • Ask happy customers for reviews and referrals

Plan by season, not month, and remember seasons are reversed in Australia and New Zealand. Spring brings startup cleanups, the growing season is your peak, fall (autumn) means leaves and tidy-ups, and winter is for hedges, servicing equipment, snow clearing where relevant and booking next season early. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a license to start a lawn mowing business?

Simple mowing often needs only business registration and any general local business license, but rules vary by area. Applying pesticides or fertilizer treatments for pay usually requires a separate license or certificate. Check noise rules for power tools and green waste disposal rules too. In the UK, carrying customers' green waste usually needs a waste carrier registration.

What equipment do I need to start a lawn care business?

Most new lawn businesses start with a reliable mower, a string trimmer, an edger, a leaf blower, hand tools, tarps and safety gear including eye and hearing protection and sturdy boots. A suitable vehicle, and later a trailer, carries it all. Buy good used equipment where sensible and add hedge trimmers or larger mowers as your services grow.

How do you price lawn mowing jobs?

Work out what each billable hour must bring in from your pay, costs and taxes, then estimate the full time a lawn takes, including travel and setup, and multiply. Many businesses charge per visit, with a minimum charge for small lawns. Price extras like hedge trimming and green waste removal separately, and check your result against local rates.

How do lawn care businesses make money in winter?

Many offer hedge trimming, leaf and tidy-up work, garden cleanups and snow clearing where it applies. Seasonal contracts paid in equal monthly amounts also smooth cash flow through quiet months. Winter is the time to service equipment, review prices and book customers for next season early, so your round is ready when growth starts again.

Is a lawn care business a good side hustle?

It can be, because you can start on weekends with modest equipment and grow street by street. You still need liability insurance, registration and records from the first paid job, and the weather will sometimes cancel your plans. Starting part time lets you learn your real job times and build a round before going full time.

Which way should you mow on a slope?

With a walk-behind mower, mow across the slope so the mower cannot roll back onto you. Ride-on or riding mowers usually go up and down slopes instead, but always follow the manufacturer's instructions. Avoid steep or wet slopes and banks near drop-offs entirely, and use a different tool or leave them out of the quote.

Your first step today is to draw your service area around home and pick the three streets closest to you. Then call an insurer for a liability quote and check your local business and noise rules. Once those are sorted, a stack of flyers on those three streets is the start of your first round.
