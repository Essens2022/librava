Fatty Liver Diet Plan: What to Eat, What to Limit and a Sample Week

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If a blood test or scan has just shown fatty liver, you are probably wondering what you should eat now. The short answer: a Mediterranean-style diet built on vegetables, beans, whole grains, fish and olive oil, with far less added sugar and sugary drinks, is the eating pattern most often recommended for fatty liver.

There is no detox tea, crash diet or banned-food list in this plan. Below you will find how to build a liver-friendly plate, what to cut back first, easy swaps, a sample week of meals and the warning signs that mean you should see a doctor quickly.

What fatty liver means in plain words

Fatty liver disease means extra fat has built up in the liver. Its newer name is MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease), and you may also see the older name NAFLD. It is linked with carrying extra weight around the middle, type 2 diabetes or prediabetes, high blood pressure and high cholesterol or triglycerides.

It is very common and often causes no symptoms, which is why it is usually found by chance. In some people the fat comes with inflammation and liver cell damage, called MASH (previously NASH), which can lead to scarring over many years. Only your doctor can tell you how advanced your own liver condition is.

The encouraging part is that daily habits matter. Research suggests that healthy eating, regular activity and, for people who are overweight, gradual weight loss can reduce liver fat and improve liver health for many people. Results vary, and your doctor checks your progress.

How to build a liver-friendly plate

You do not need to weigh food or count anything. Picture a dinner plate about 9 to 10 inches (23 to 25 cm) across:

  • Half the plate: vegetables or salad, cooked or raw, the more colors the better.
  • A quarter: whole grains, potatoes with skins, or beans and lentils.
  • A quarter: protein, such as fish, chicken, eggs, tofu or beans.
  • A drizzle of olive oil, a few olives or a sprinkle of nuts, plus herbs and lemon.
  • Water or another unsweetened drink alongside.

Your hand is a decent portion guide. A portion of meat or fish is about the size and thickness of your palm. Cooked grains or pasta are about a cupped handful, or 1/2 to 3/4 cup (around 100 to 150 g cooked). Cheese is about two thumbs, and nuts fit in a cupped palm. Vegetables have no real upper limit.

Eat slowly, put your fork down between mouthfuls and stop when you are comfortably full. Three regular meals usually work better than skipping meals all day and eating a huge dinner.

Cut added sugar and sugary drinks first

For many people with fatty liver, cutting back on added sugar, and especially sugary drinks, is the single most helpful change. The liver turns extra sugar, particularly fructose from added sugars, into fat. A large sugary drink delivers a lot of sugar fast without filling you up, so it is easy to have several a day without noticing.

Added sugar also hides in ketchup, barbecue sauce, sweet chili sauce, jarred pasta sauces, salad dressings, flavored yogurts, cereals, granola, cereal bars, bread and ready meals. On labels, look for names such as glucose, fructose, sucrose, dextrose, corn syrup, glucose syrup, honey, agave, molasses and fruit juice concentrate.

You do not have to quit everything on Monday. Replace one sugary drink a day for a week, then two. Slow change tends to stick.

Best foods to eat for fatty liver

The Mediterranean-style pattern is not a strict diet. It is a way of filling your plate most days, and research links it with less liver fat and better heart health, even when weight changes only a little.

  • Plenty of vegetables and whole fruit. Whole fruit is encouraged; it brings fiber and fills you up.
  • Beans, lentils and chickpeas several times a week.
  • Whole grains such as oats, brown rice, barley, bulgur, quinoa and whole wheat (wholemeal) bread and pasta.
  • Fish about twice a week, including oily fish such as salmon, sardines, mackerel and trout.
  • Chicken, eggs and plain yogurt in moderate amounts.
  • Olive oil as your main fat, plus a small handful (about 1 oz / 30 g) of nuts or seeds most days.
  • Herbs, spices, garlic and lemon in place of extra salt.

Olive oil is still rich, so measure it with a spoon rather than pouring from the bottle. One to two tablespoons for a dish serving four is plenty for most meals.

Foods to limit with fatty liver

Fat is not banned, but the type matters. Refined starches are digested quickly and, over time, diets heavy in them are linked with more liver fat. Cut back on:

  • white bread, pastries, crackers, many breakfast cereals and large portions of white rice or pasta
  • butter, lard and hard cooking fats
  • processed meats such as bacon, sausages, ham and salami, and red meat beyond now and then
  • fried food, takeaway fries (chips), pies and many packaged snacks
  • salty foods: most adults should stay below about 6 g of salt (2,300 mg sodium) a day, roughly one level teaspoon in total

Two easy meal ideas

Greek chicken tray bake: toss chicken thighs, peppers, red onion, zucchini (courgette) and cherry tomatoes with olive oil, lemon, garlic and oregano, then roast until the chicken reaches 165°F (74°C). Serve with a big salad.

Chickpea and spinach stew: soften onion and garlic in olive oil, add cumin, canned tomatoes and rinsed chickpeas, simmer for 15 minutes and stir in spinach. A cupped handful of brown rice or a slice of whole grain bread completes the plate.

Alcohol, coffee and the liver detox myth

Alcohol is processed by the liver and adds to the strain on a liver already storing extra fat. People with fatty liver are generally advised to limit alcohol, and many doctors advise avoiding it completely. Anyone with liver scarring (fibrosis or cirrhosis) should not drink at all. Follow your own doctor's advice.

If you have been drinking heavily every day for a long time, do not stop suddenly on your own. Withdrawal can be dangerous, so talk to your doctor first and cut down with support.

Some studies suggest coffee drinkers may have lower liver risks, but coffee is not a treatment and there is no need to start. If you drink it, have it black or with a little milk, not loaded with syrup and cream.

Your liver cleans itself. No tea, juice, pill or cleanse scrubs fat out of it, and some herbal and dietary supplements, including some sold for liver health, weight loss or bodybuilding and high-dose green tea extract, can actually harm the liver. Tell your doctor and pharmacist about every supplement and herbal remedy you take.

Weight loss and exercise for fatty liver

If you are overweight, losing weight gradually often helps the liver. Studies suggest that losing even a modest amount, roughly 5 to 10 percent of body weight, can reduce liver fat. Your doctor can help you choose a sensible target.

  • Aim for no more than about 1 to 2 lb (0.5 to 1 kg) a week.
  • Avoid crash diets, very low calorie diets and fasting regimes without medical supervision; very fast weight loss can be hard on the liver.
  • If your weight is already in a healthy range, the eating pattern and activity still help.

Activity helps the liver use fat and sugar better, even without weight loss. Adults are generally advised to build up to about 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking, plus muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days, like carrying shopping, gardening or standing up from a chair repeatedly.

Start small. A 10 minute walk after dinner is a fine first step, and it also helps your body handle the sugar from that meal. If you sit a lot for work, get up and move for a few minutes every hour or so.

Warning signs and working with your doctor

Fatty liver is usually quiet, but you should know the signs that something more serious may be happening. Regular checkups (blood tests, blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol) help your doctor track your liver, and it is worth asking about vaccination against hepatitis A and B.

Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before big changes to your diet or exercise, and never stop or change prescribed medicines because of diet. If you take insulin or diabetes tablets that can cause low blood sugar, check before cutting carbohydrates. People with cirrhosis, kidney disease, pregnancy or a history of disordered eating need personal advice. Get urgent help for yellowing of the skin or eyes, vomiting blood or material like coffee grounds, black or tarry stools, sudden swelling of the abdomen or legs, confusion or unusual drowsiness, or severe abdominal pain or fever with abdominal pain. Call 911 (US, Canada), 999 (UK), 000 (Australia) or 111 (New Zealand).

See a doctor soon, not urgently but without waiting months, for ongoing tiredness, itchy skin, dark urine, pale stools, easy bruising or unexplained weight loss.

Frequently asked questions

What foods are bad for fatty liver?

The foods most worth limiting are sugary drinks, foods with lots of added sugar, refined starches such as white bread and pastries, fried food, processed meats and very salty ready meals. Alcohol adds strain too, and many doctors advise avoiding it. No single food causes fatty liver, so focus on your overall pattern: more vegetables, beans, whole grains, fish and olive oil, and fewer ultra-processed foods.

Can fatty liver be reversed with diet?

Research suggests that healthy eating, regular activity and, for people who are overweight, gradual weight loss can reduce liver fat and improve liver health for many people. Results vary from person to person, and nobody can promise a cure. Your doctor checks your progress with blood tests or scans, so keep up your checkups and follow their advice alongside your new habits.

Is fruit bad for fatty liver?

No. Whole fruit is encouraged. It comes with fiber, water and vitamins, takes time to chew and fills you up. The concern is added sugar and large amounts of juice or smoothies, which deliver the sugar of several fruits quickly. Enjoy whole fruit as snacks, and if you like juice, keep it to a small glass, about 5 fl oz (150 ml), with a meal.

What is the best drink for fatty liver?

Water is the best everyday drink. Sparkling water with a slice of lemon or cucumber, unsweetened tea (hot or iced), and black coffee or coffee with milk are all good choices. Cut back on soda (fizzy drinks), sweetened coffee shop drinks, energy drinks and large glasses of juice. Diet drinks can be a stepping stone, but water and unsweetened drinks are the better habit.

How long does it take to improve fatty liver?

It varies. Changes in liver fat build gradually with steady habits over months rather than weeks, and everyone responds differently. Slow weight loss of about 1 to 2 lb (0.5 to 1 kg) a week at most, if you need it, plus regular activity and better food choices, gives your liver the best chance. Your doctor will decide when to repeat blood tests or scans.

Are eggs OK with fatty liver?

Yes, for most people eggs fit a liver-friendly diet in moderate amounts. They are a handy protein for breakfast or a quick dinner, such as a vegetable omelet or eggs baked with tomatoes. Cook them with olive oil rather than butter, and serve them with vegetables and whole grain bread instead of bacon or sausages. Ask your doctor if you have been told to limit cholesterol.

Your first step today is simple: pick the one sugary drink you have most days and replace it with water, sparkling water or unsweetened tea, then take a 10 minute walk after dinner and build from there with your doctor's support.
