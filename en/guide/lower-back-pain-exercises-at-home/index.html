Lower Back Pain Exercises at Home: A Gentle Plan for Stiff Backs

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If your lower back aches every morning, or it grabbed you while you were lifting the shopping, you are in very good company. The short answer is that gentle movement usually helps more than rest: a few slow stretches such as pelvic tilts, knee to chest and cat and cow, simple strength moves like bridges and bird dog, and short daily walks, all done within comfort and built up slowly.

This guide walks you through each exercise step by step, shows you how many to do, gives you a 10-minute morning routine and a 15-minute strength routine, and ends with a four-week plan. It also lists the warning signs that mean you should get help instead.

Is it safe to exercise with lower back pain?

For most everyday lower back pain, yes. Most back pain is not caused by serious damage, and it usually improves within a few weeks. How much it hurts is not a reliable guide to how much is wrong.

The general advice is to stay as active as you can and return to normal activities gradually. Long spells in bed tend to leave you stiffer, weaker and more worried, which can slow recovery. Gentle movement keeps muscles from locking up and shows your nervous system that moving is safe.

Every back is different. In the first day or two of a bad flare up, stick to short walks and comfortable resting positions, then add the exercises below once you can move about fairly easily.

Check first. This is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor (GP), physical therapist (UK: physiotherapist) or pharmacist before starting new exercises or medicines, especially if you are pregnant, have osteoporosis, have had recent surgery or have a known spine condition, or if your pain is severe, new or getting worse.

Warning signs to check before you start

A few symptoms mean exercise is not the right next step. They are rare, but they matter.

Go to the emergency department (UK: A&E) or call emergency services straight away (911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK, 000 in Australia, 111 in New Zealand) if you have:

  • Numbness or tingling around your genitals, buttocks or inner thighs (the area that would touch a saddle)
  • New loss of bladder or bowel control, or new difficulty peeing

See a doctor urgently, ideally the same day, if you have:

  • Weakness in one or both legs, or legs that give way, or numbness in both legs
  • Back pain with a fever or feeling very unwell
  • Back pain after a fall, car accident or other injury
  • Back pain with a history of cancer, or unexplained weight loss
  • Severe, constant pain that is worse at night and does not ease with rest or changing position
  • Chest pain, or pain with shortness of breath

Book a regular appointment if pain lasts more than a few weeks, keeps getting worse, stops you doing daily tasks, keeps coming back, spreads down your leg below the knee, or simply worries you.

How to do back exercises safely

The exercises here are meant to feel like a mild pull or a bit of honest effort, never a fight. A few simple rules keep them comfortable:

  • Move slowly into each position and slowly out of it. No bouncing.
  • Breathe normally. If you catch yourself holding your breath, ease off.
  • Start small, with about 3 to 5 repetitions, and build up over days and weeks.
  • Change only one thing at a time: a couple more repetitions, a slightly longer hold or an extra set.
  • Expect some days to feel stiffer than others, and adjust.

Mild stretching discomfort and tired muscles the next day are normal. Stop and get advice if you feel sharp pain, pain that spreads down your leg, numbness, tingling or weakness.

You need a mat or folded blanket, a towel, a cushion and a sturdy chair. To get up from the floor, roll onto your side, push up to your hands and knees and use the chair to stand.

Gentle stretches for lower back pain

These stretches loosen a stiff back and are a good place to start.

Pelvic tilt

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip width apart.
  2. Breathe out and gently tighten your belly so your lower back flattens toward the floor.
  3. Hold for 2 to 3 seconds, then relax. Do 5 to 8, building to 10.

Knee to chest

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent.
  2. Hold one knee behind the thigh and draw it gently toward your chest until you feel a mild stretch in your lower back or buttock.
  3. Hold for 15 to 20 seconds, lower slowly and switch. Do 2 to 3 each side. Try both knees together once one feels easy.

Lying knee rolls

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent and together, arms out to the sides.
  2. Keeping your shoulders down, let both knees roll slowly to one side, only as far as is comfortable.
  3. Pause for a breath, return to the middle and roll the other way. Do 5 to 8 each side.

Cat and cow

  1. Start on your hands and knees, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
  2. Breathe out and round your back toward the ceiling, letting your head drop.
  3. Breathe in and let your belly lower gently, lifting your head a little. Keep the arch small. Do 5 to 10 slow rounds.

Child's pose with knees wide

From hands and knees, move your knees wide, sit your hips back toward your heels and let your chest lower, with a cushion under your chest or forehead if you like. Stay for 20 to 30 seconds. Skip it if it bothers your knees or back.

Hamstring and hip flexor stretches

Lying on your back, loop a towel around one foot and slowly raise that leg until the back of the thigh feels a mild stretch. For the front of the hip, half kneel on a cushion beside a chair, tuck your tailbone under and shift gently forward. Hold each for 20 to 30 seconds.

Strengthening exercises for your lower back and core

Your belly, buttocks, hips and legs all share the load when you lift, bend and carry. When they are stronger, everyday tasks ask less of your back. Studies suggest exercise is one of the most helpful things people can do for everyday back pain, and there is no single best exercise: the best one is the one you keep doing.

Bridges

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
  2. Tighten your belly, squeeze your buttocks and lift your hips until your body is a straight line from shoulders to knees.
  3. Hold for 2 to 3 seconds and lower slowly. Start with 5 to 8.

Bird dog

  1. On hands and knees, keep your back flat like a tabletop.
  2. Slide one leg back and lift it roughly in line with your body, hips level.
  3. Hold for 2 to 3 seconds and return. Later, lift the opposite arm at the same time. Start with 4 to 5 each side.

Dead bug

Lie on your back with knees bent above your hips and arms reaching up. Keeping your lower back close to the floor, slowly lower one heel toward the floor and bring it back, then switch. If your back starts to arch, make the movement smaller.

Sit to stand

Sit near the front of a sturdy chair, feet a little behind your knees. Lean forward until your nose is over your toes, push through your legs to stand, then sit back down slowly instead of dropping. Start with 5. This may be the most useful exercise of all, because you do it many times a day.

Side lying leg lifts and short wall sits round out the set.

A 10-minute morning stretch routine

Backs are often stiffest straight out of bed, so get up and move around for a few minutes first. Then try this:

To see if it helps, rate your stiffness from 0 to 10 before and after each session for a week.

Is walking good for lower back pain?

Walking is one of the best things you can do. It moves the joints and muscles of your back gently, and for many people it feels better than sitting or lying for long periods.

On a bad day, walk to the kitchen and back every hour or two while you are awake. As things ease, stretch it to 5 minutes, then 10.

Over time, adults are generally advised to aim for about 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus strength work on two days. You do not need to start there.

Avoid doing loads on a good day and paying for it for three days. A steady amount every day works better.

If you are coming out of a flare up or have been inactive for a while, this gives you a calm structure. Repeat a week if it feels like too much.

Small daily habits help the exercises work. Change position at least every 30 minutes or so, since there is no single perfect posture and the best position is usually the next one. When lifting, keep the load close, bend your knees and hips, and turn with your feet instead of twisting. At night, try lying on your side with a pillow between your knees, or on your back with a pillow under them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best exercise for lower back pain?

There is no single best exercise for everyone. A simple mix works well for most people with everyday back pain: gentle stretches such as pelvic tilts and knee to chest, strength moves such as bridges and bird dog, and regular walking. The most helpful program is the one you can do comfortably and keep doing most days. Start small and build up slowly.

How often should I do lower back exercises?

Gentle stretches can be done daily, for example a 5 to 10 minute routine each morning. Strength exercises work well two or three times a week with a rest day between sessions. Add walking on most days. Short and regular beats long and occasional, so a few minutes every day will usually help more than one big session at the weekend.

Should I rest or keep moving with lower back pain?

For most everyday back pain, keep moving gently. Staying as active as you can, within comfort, tends to help recovery more than bed rest, which can leave you stiffer and weaker. Short rests of 20 to 30 minutes in a comfortable position are fine, then get up and move again. Check the warning signs first and get help if any apply.

Which exercises should I avoid with lower back pain?

Avoid anything that causes sharp pain, pain spreading down your leg, numbness or tingling. During a flare up, skip heavy lifting, fast twisting, bouncing stretches and long sessions on low, soft sofas. Backward bends such as a press up on your front suit some people and not others, so try them gently and stop if pain increases. Return to harder activities gradually.

How long does it take for back exercises to help?

Many people notice that stretches ease morning stiffness within a week or two, while strength work takes several weeks to build. Everyday back pain often improves within a few weeks anyway, and exercise supports that and may make future flare ups less likely. If pain lasts more than a few weeks or keeps getting worse, see a doctor or physical therapist.

Can I do back exercises in bed?

Yes, many of them. Pelvic tilts, knee to chest, lying knee rolls, the towel hamstring stretch and even bridges work on a firm bed, which helps if getting down to the floor is hard. Move slowly and breathe normally. To get out of bed afterward, roll onto your side, let your legs slide off the edge and push up with your arms.

Your first step today is small: after you have been up for a few minutes tomorrow morning, do five pelvic tilts, two knee to chest stretches on each side and five knee rolls, then go for a short walk. Notice how your back feels before and after, and build from there one small change at a time.
