Acid Reflux Diet: What to Eat, Common Triggers and Calmer Nights

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If heartburn keeps waking you up or spoiling your evenings, you are probably wondering what you should be eating. The short answer: smaller meals, finishing dinner 2 to 3 hours before bed and gently cooked food built on lean protein, vegetables and grains help many people, while common triggers such as fatty food, alcohol, coffee and tomatoes are worth testing one at a time rather than banning all at once.

This guide explains why reflux happens, which foods tend to sit lightly, which ones commonly cause trouble, the habits that often matter more than the menu, a simple way to find your own triggers and the warning signs that need a doctor quickly.

Why acid reflux happens

At the bottom of your esophagus (oesophagus), the food pipe, sits a ring of muscle that works like a one-way valve. It opens to let food into the stomach and closes to keep strong stomach acid where it belongs. Reflux happens when that valve relaxes at the wrong moment or is under pressure from below, so stomach contents wash back up.

Plenty of everyday things add that pressure: a very full stomach, lying down soon after eating, extra weight around the middle, tight waistbands, bending over and pregnancy. Heartburn is the burning feeling behind the breastbone that reflux often causes. Other signs include a sour taste, food coming back up, hoarseness, a nagging cough and needing to clear your throat a lot.

Occasional heartburn after a big holiday dinner is common. Reflux twice a week or more, or reflux that affects your eating, sleep or daily life, may be GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease, written gastro-oesophageal in the UK), which needs a doctor's assessment, not just a change of menu.

Foods that are often easier on reflux

There is no single acid reflux diet that works for everyone, but certain foods tend to sit lightly for many people:

  • Oats, rice, quinoa, potatoes, sweet potatoes, pasta and whole grain bread.
  • Lean proteins: skinless chicken or turkey, white fish, salmon in sensible portions, eggs, tofu, beans and lentils.
  • Gentle vegetables: carrots, green beans, zucchini (courgette), spinach, peas, squash, broccoli, cucumber and fennel.
  • Lower-fat milk and plain yogurt, if dairy suits you.
  • Fresh herbs, fresh ginger, toasted seeds and mild spices such as cumin, ground coriander, sweet paprika and turmeric.

How you cook matters as much as what you cook. Bake, roast, poach, steam or sauté lightly with a teaspoon or two of oil instead of frying. Poaching chicken or fish in a gently simmering broth (stock) keeps it moist with no added fat, and oven baking gives breaded chicken a crisp crust without a deep fryer.

Do not strip out every bit of fat. A little olive oil carries flavor and keeps meals satisfying. Modest fat is the aim, not none at all.

Foods to avoid with acid reflux

These are the most commonly reported triggers. The evidence that avoiding each one helps everyone is mixed, so treat this as a list of suspects, not a list of rules.

Common triggerGentler swap to try
Fried chicken or fishOven-baked with a crumb coating
Tomato pasta sauceRoasted red pepper and carrot sauce
Chili heat and spicy foodGinger, cumin, sweet paprika
Raw garlic and onionChives, scallion (spring onion) greens, herbs
Lemon or vinegar dressingHerb yogurt, or olive oil and herbs

Chocolate, mint and alcoholTest each one; keep what your body tolerates

Fatty and fried foods slow down how fast the stomach empties. Alcohol can relax the valve and irritate the esophagus. Peppermint, often sold as a stomach soother, is a common trigger too. Cutting out everything is not necessary or helpful: it makes meals dull and eating out stressful, and can leave you short of variety. Avoid only what genuinely bothers you.

Habits that matter as much as food

The clearest culprits are often how and when you eat rather than exactly what. These habits help many people:

  • The 2 to 3 hour rule. Stop eating 2 to 3 hours before you lie down or go to bed; some people need 3 to 4. If you go to bed at 10:30, finish dinner by 7:30 or 8:00.
  • Eat smaller meals, slowly. Three modest meals and one or two light snacks put less pressure on the valve than a skipped lunch and a huge dinner. Put your fork down between bites and wait 10 minutes before seconds.
  • Raise the head of your bed by 4 to 8 inches (10 to 20 cm) with sturdy blocks under the legs or a wedge under the mattress. Extra pillows bend you at the waist and can make things worse.
  • Try sleeping on your left side, which some people find helps.
  • Loosen tight belts and waistbands after meals.
  • Stop smoking, keep alcohol modest and well before bedtime, and if you carry extra weight around the middle, gradual weight change helps many people.

Naps count too. Lying flat on the sofa straight after Sunday lunch is a classic heartburn moment, so take a short walk or sit upright first.

What to drink with acid reflux

Drinks are easy to overlook. Coffee and other caffeinated drinks (strong tea, cola, energy drinks), fizzy drinks, alcohol, citrus juices and mint tea are all commonly reported triggers. You may not react to all of them.

Gentler choices include plain water, still water with a slice of cucumber, caffeine-free herbal teas such as chamomile, rooibos or ginger (not mint), weak or decaf tea, and small amounts of decaf coffee if you tolerate it. A lower-fat milk is easier than full-fat for many people. Sip through the day rather than gulping a large glass with a meal.

If coffee is your weak spot, you may not need to quit. One small cup with breakfast and none after noon works for plenty of people. Your diary will tell you.

How to find your own reflux triggers

Triggers are personal. One person reacts to coffee and not tomatoes, the next is the opposite. A short diary replaces guesswork with your own evidence.

  1. For 2 to 4 weeks, note each time you eat or drink: the time, what and roughly how much, your position afterward (sitting, walking, lying down), any symptoms on a scale of 1 to 5, and notes on stress, sleep or tight clothes.
  2. Change nothing in the first week. You are collecting information.
  3. At the end of each week, look for repeats. Did symptoms follow large meals, late dinners or a particular drink?
  4. Test one suspect at a time. Leave it out for 1 to 2 weeks, then bring back a normal portion at lunch and watch the next few hours. Repeat a couple of times.
  5. If symptoms return each time, you have likely found a real trigger. If not, welcome that food back.

Test portion size as well as the food. A little onion in soup may be fine while a plate of fried onion rings is not. Bring the diary to your doctor; it turns "I get heartburn a lot" into something they can work with.

A sample day of reflux-friendly meals

Here is how a gentle day might look for someone who goes to bed around 10:30 p.m. Adjust the times to your schedule; the pattern matters more than the clock.

TimeMeal
7:00 a.m.Savory oatmeal (porridge) with a poached egg and spinach
10:30 a.m.Rice cakes with cottage cheese and cucumber
12:30 p.m.Main meal: herb roasted chicken with root vegetables and rice
3:30 p.m.Plain yogurt with a few oats, or hummus made with little garlic and carrot sticks
6:30 to 7:30 p.m.Light dinner: carrot and red lentil soup with bread

Shift workers can follow the same rhythm by treating sleep time as night, keeping the last meal small and finishing it 2 to 3 hours before bed.

If you miss pasta night, try a tomato-free sauce. Roast red bell peppers and carrots, blend them with a little broth (stock), then finish with grated Parmesan, sweet paprika and fresh basil. Heartburn remedies and online cures

Pharmacy remedies can help in the short term. Antacids neutralize acid already in the stomach, alginates form a layer on top of stomach contents, and acid-reducing tablets lower how much acid you make. Ask a pharmacist which suits you, especially if you take other medicines, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have another condition.

Many kitchen cures are not proven. Baking soda (bicarbonate of soda) in water is high in sodium and can be risky for people with heart or kidney problems or who are pregnant. There is no good evidence that apple cider vinegar helps, and it is acidic. Cold milk may feel soothing for a moment but is not a treatment.

Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before big diet changes, especially if you have diabetes, kidney disease, celiac (coeliac) disease, food allergies or a history of disordered eating. Never stop or change a prescribed medicine because you feel better after changing your diet. See your doctor if you have heartburn most days for 3 weeks or more, need pharmacy remedies for more than about 2 weeks, symptoms wake you at night, or you are over 55 with new or changed symptoms.

Red flags that need urgent help

Most heartburn is uncomfortable rather than dangerous, but some symptoms need medical help the same day:

  • Difficulty swallowing, food sticking on the way down or pain when swallowing.
  • Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds.
  • Black, sticky or tarry stools.
  • Weight loss you cannot explain, repeated vomiting or a lump in your stomach area.

Heartburn and a heart attack can feel surprisingly alike. Call emergency services straight away (911 in the US, 999 in the UK) if you have chest pain or pressure that spreads to your arm, neck, jaw or back, or comes with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or feeling faint. If you are ever unsure about chest pain, treat it as an emergency.

Frequently asked questions

What foods help acid reflux go away?

No food makes reflux go away, but many people find oats, rice, potatoes, lean chicken and fish, eggs, gentle vegetables such as carrots and green beans, and fresh ginger sit lightly. Cooking by baking, poaching or steaming instead of frying helps too. Pair these with smaller meals and an earlier dinner, which often make the biggest difference. If symptoms persist, see your doctor.

What is the best thing to drink for acid reflux?

Plain water is the simplest choice. Still water with cucumber, caffeine-free herbal teas such as chamomile, rooibos or ginger, and weak or decaf tea suit many people. Coffee, fizzy drinks, alcohol, citrus juice and mint tea are common triggers, though not for everyone. Sip drinks through the day rather than drinking a large glass with a meal, which can add to that overfull feeling.

How do I stop acid reflux at night?

Finish eating 2 to 3 hours before bed, keep dinner small and light, and avoid lying down after meals. Raise the head of your bed by 4 to 8 inches (10 to 20 cm) with blocks or a wedge under the mattress rather than extra pillows. Some people find sleeping on the left side helps. If night symptoms keep waking you, see your doctor.

Are bananas good for acid reflux?

Bananas are a gentle, low-acid fruit that many people with reflux tolerate well, so they are a reasonable snack to try. Triggers are personal, though, and a few people do notice symptoms after bananas. The simplest way to know is to note how you feel in a food and symptom diary for a couple of weeks, and keep any food that does not cause you trouble.

Is milk good for heartburn?

Cold milk can feel soothing for a moment, but it is not a treatment, and full-fat milk can leave some people feeling worse later because fat slows stomach emptying. If dairy suits you, lower-fat milk and plain yogurt are usually easier. For quick relief, ask a pharmacist about a suitable antacid or alginate rather than relying on milk.

How long does it take for a reflux diet to work?

Habits such as an earlier, smaller dinner can make a difference within a week or two for many people, which is why a 7 day earlier dinner trial is a good start. Finding food triggers takes longer, usually 2 to 4 weeks of keeping a diary and testing one food at a time. If you still have heartburn most days after 3 weeks, see your doctor.

Your first step today: set a "kitchen closed" reminder for 3 hours before your usual bedtime and keep to it for the next 7 nights, noting how you feel in a simple diary. Once that habit is in place, start testing suspected trigger foods one at a time, and keep everything your body handles well on the menu.
