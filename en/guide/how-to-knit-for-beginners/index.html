How to Knit for Beginners: Cast On, Knit, Purl and Bind Off

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

Maybe you learned as a child and forgot, or you have never held a pair of needles and feel all thumbs. To knit as a beginner you need just four skills: casting on, the knit stitch, the purl stitch and binding off, practiced on smooth, light-colored worsted (aran) yarn and US 8 (5 mm) needles.

With those four you can already make scarves, dishcloths and blankets. This guide covers what to buy, each step in plain words, a first project you can finish in a weekend, and how to fix the mistakes every beginner makes.

What you need to start knitting

Your starter kit is small and inexpensive.

  • One ball of worsted weight yarn (aran in the UK, 10-ply in Australia and New Zealand), about 100 g and at least 150 yd (137 m). Choose smooth wool or a wool blend in a light, solid color.
  • A pair of US 8 (5 mm) straight needles, about 10 in (25 cm) long. Wood or bamboo grips the stitches, so they slide off less easily than on metal.
  • Small scissors.
  • A yarn needle (a large, blunt needle with a big eye) for weaving in ends.

Skip fuzzy, dark, multicolored or novelty yarns for now. They are lovely later, but they hide your stitches, and seeing your stitches is how you learn. Yarn that looks like loose strands lying side by side splits easily, so avoid that too.

How to make a slip knot and measure the tail

Nearly every cast on starts with a slip knot, a loop that tightens when you pull one end. It counts as your first stitch.

  1. Cross the yarn coming from the ball over the tail end to make a circle.
  2. Reach through the circle from front to back and grab the ball yarn just past it.
  3. Pull a small loop of ball yarn up through the circle so the circle closes around it.
  4. Put the loop on a needle and pull the ball end until it sits snugly but can still slide.

For the long-tail cast on below, you need enough tail for every stitch. Allow about 1 in (2.5 cm) per stitch in worsted yarn plus about 6 in (15 cm) extra. For 20 stitches, make your slip knot about 26 in (66 cm) from the end. If you would rather not measure, wrap the yarn loosely around the needle once per stitch, add 6 in (15 cm), and make the knot there.

How to cast on with the long-tail method

The long-tail cast on gives a neat, firm, stretchy edge and suits most projects. It looks like a string game at first, but your fingers learn it within about 20 stitches.

Set up

  1. Hold the needle with the slip knot in your right hand, index finger resting on the knot.
  2. Let both strands hang down, with the tail nearer you.
  3. Slide your left thumb and index finger between the strands from below: tail over the thumb, ball yarn over the index finger. Close your other fingers over both strands.
  4. Turn your left palm up and spread thumb and finger apart like a slingshot, so the yarn makes a V.

Make each stitch

  1. Take the needle tip up through the loop around your thumb, from underneath.
  2. Swing it over to catch the strand running from your index finger.
  3. Bring it back down through the thumb loop, carrying that strand.
  4. Drop the loop off your thumb, then put your thumb back under the tail and gently spread to snug the stitch. Do not pull it tight.

Leave about a needle's width between stitches. Crammed stitches make a tight edge, and big gaps make it loopy. Practice by casting on 20, pulling them off and doing it again twice. If the long-tail method frustrates you, the knitted cast on, which uses the knit stitch itself, is a fine alternative.

How to do the knit stitch step by step

This is English style, where your right hand wraps the yarn. Keep the working yarn at the back, on the side away from you.

  1. Push the right needle into the front of the first stitch on the left needle, from left to right, so the needles cross with the right one behind.
  2. Steady the crossed needles with your left hand.
  3. Wrap the working yarn around the right needle counterclockwise, under and then over the tip, so it lies between the needles.
  4. Draw the right needle down and toward you through the old stitch, bringing the wrap with it as a new loop.
  5. Slide the old stitch off the left needle. The new stitch stays on the right.

A classic rhyme keeps the order straight: "In through the front door, once around the back, out through the window, and off jumps Jack." When all the stitches are on the right needle, swap needles between hands and start the next row.

In continental style, the left hand holds the yarn and the right needle picks it up instead of wrapping. It is often a little faster. Neither style is more correct, so use whichever feels natural. Knitting every row creates garter stitch, a squishy, ridged fabric that lies flat and is ideal for first projects.

How to purl and make stockinette stitch

The purl stitch is the knit stitch seen from the other side. Practice it once knitting feels comfortable.

  1. Bring the working yarn to the front, between the needle tips.
  2. Insert the right needle into the front of the first stitch from right to left, so it sits in front of the left needle.
  3. Wrap the yarn over the top of the right needle from front to back, then down between the needles toward you.
  4. Push the right needle back and down through the old stitch, taking the new loop to the back.
  5. Slide the old stitch off. Keep the yarn in front for the next one.

Knit one row and purl the next, and you get stockinette stitch (stocking stitch in the UK): smooth columns of Vs on the right side and bumps on the wrong side. It is the fabric of most sweaters and hats, but it curls at the edges, so patterns add a border of garter stitch or ribbing.

To see which row comes next, look at the stitches on your left needle. A smooth V facing you means knit, and a little bump like a collar means purl. Moving the yarn between the needles (not over them) when you switch between knit and purl stops extra stitches appearing.

How to bind off knitting

Binding off (casting off in the UK) locks your stitches so they cannot unravel.

  1. Knit two stitches, so two sit on the right needle.
  2. With the left needle tip, lift the first of those two stitches over the second and off the needle.
  3. Knit one more stitch, and lift the previous stitch over it again.
  4. Repeat until one stitch is left on the right needle.
  5. Cut the yarn leaving about 6 in (15 cm), pull the last loop larger, remove the needle and pull the tail through to lock it.

Keep it loose. A tight bind off pulls the top edge narrower than the rest. If yours tends to be tight, use a needle one or two sizes larger in your right hand just for that row. Finish by threading each tail onto the yarn needle and weaving it through the back of several stitches before trimming.

Easy first knitting project to try

A garter stitch dishcloth is small, useful and finished in about 3 to 4 hours spread over a weekend.

  • Yarn: about 80 yd (73 m) of worsted/aran weight cotton in a light color
  • Needles: US 7 (4.5 mm)
  • Size: about 8 in (20 cm) square
  1. Leave a tail of about 44 in (1.1 m) and cast on 38 stitches.
  2. Knit every row until the piece measures about 8 in (20 cm), roughly 72 rows or 36 ridges.
  3. Check it is square by folding one bottom corner up to the opposite side. A neat triangle means you are done.
  4. Count your stitches (still 38?), bind off loosely and weave in both ends.

Cotton has little stretch and can tire your fingers sooner than wool, so keep your grip relaxed. After that, a chunky garter scarf on bigger needles is a satisfying second project. Fewer stitchesA stitch slipped off unworkedFind the ladder and pick it up
Twisted, crossed stitchesStitch sits with its front leg at the backTurn it around on the needle before working it

Picking up a dropped stitch

Catch the loose loop with a safety pin so it cannot run further. Work across to where it belongs. With the knit side facing you, put a crochet hook through the loop from the front, catch the lowest horizontal bar above it and pull it through. Repeat bar by bar to the top without skipping any, then put the loop back on the left needle with its front leg toward the tip.

Keep stitches about 1 in (2.5 cm) back from the left needle tip and nudge them forward as you go. It stops them sliding off, especially at the end of a row.

Short, regular sessions beat long marathons. Three or four 20-minute sessions a week, in a comfortable chair with good light and your arms supported, will get your hands fluent faster than one long Sunday. Take breaks, stretch your hands and wrists, and stop if pain persists rather than pushing through.

Accept that your first swatch will look uneven. Tension settles with practice, and a wash softens and evens out early stitches.

Frequently asked questions

Is knitting hard to learn?

No, though the first hour feels clumsy for nearly everyone. Most beginners can cast on and knit a few rows in their first session, and purl within a week of short practice. Using smooth, light worsted yarn and US 8 (5 mm) needles makes it much easier, because you can see each stitch clearly.

What is the easiest thing to knit first?

Anything flat in garter stitch, where you knit every row. A dishcloth of about 38 stitches in cotton is small and quick, and a chunky scarf on large needles grows fast and feels rewarding. Both use only casting on, the knit stitch and binding off, so you can concentrate on even tension.

Should I learn English or continental knitting?

Either works, and neither is more correct. In English style your right hand wraps the yarn around the needle, while continental holds the yarn in the left hand and picks it with the needle, which many find quicker. Try both for a few rows and keep the one that feels natural.

Why is my knitting getting wider?

You are adding stitches by accident, usually at the start of a row where the yarn goes up over the needle and the first stitch looks like two loops, or when the yarn ends up in front between stitches. Count your stitches every few rows, keep the yarn hanging down at the back, and knit two together once to correct.

What size knitting needles should a beginner use?

US 8 (5 mm) straight needles, about 10 in (25 cm) long, paired with worsted or aran (10-ply) yarn. That combination is thick enough to see and thin enough to handle comfortably. Wood or bamboo needles hold stitches a little more securely than slippery metal ones, which helps while you learn.

How long does it take to learn to knit?

With three or four 20-minute sessions a week, most beginners are comfortable with knit and purl within two to three weeks, and finish a dishcloth or scarf within the first month. Even tension takes longer, often a few projects, but it improves steadily with every row you work.

Your first step today is to pick up one ball of light-colored worsted yarn and a pair of US 8 (5 mm) needles, then practice casting on 20 stitches three times until your hands remember the moves. Once that feels easy, knit a few rows, and you are on your way to your first dishcloth.
