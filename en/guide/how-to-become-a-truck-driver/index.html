How to Become a Truck Driver: Licence, Training and First Job Steps

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

Maybe your warehouse job has stalled, your factory closed, or you have always liked the idea of the open road and want to know what it really takes. To become a truck driver you choose a licence class, pass a commercial driver medical, get a learner permit or provisional entitlement, complete approved training, pass the knowledge and practical tests, then apply for your first job with a clean driving record.

The order and the names change by country, and the rules change over time, so always check your national or state licensing agency for the current version. This guide lays out the full path, the costs to plan for in general terms, and the traps that catch new drivers.

How to become a truck driver in 7 steps

  1. Research and decide: talk to working drivers, talk with your family about time away and get a copy of your driving record.
  2. Check your health: book an eye test, see your doctor about any conditions or medications and pass the commercial driver medical.
  3. Choose training: compare at least three options and read any contract carefully.
  4. Get learner status and study the official handbook for the knowledge or theory tests.
  5. Train and test: practice inspections, backing and road driving, then pass the practical tests.
  6. Find a job: prepare a short resume (CV), talk to recruiters and compare offers.
  7. Start work: complete orientation, drive with a trainer or mentor and keep learning.

Many people go from first research to first paid load in a few months, but it depends heavily on where you live, the training route you choose and how quickly you can book tests.

What truck drivers really do all day

Driving is only part of the job. A typical day includes a pre-trip inspection, waiting at docks, checking paperwork, planning fuel and parking, and keeping your logbook accurate. Some work is physical too: climbing, strapping loads and pulling tarps.

Pay varies by region, type of work and experience, and nobody can honestly promise you a figure before you start. First-year work often means long hours, less choice of routes and more time away. Talk this through with your partner or family early, because it affects them as much as you.

CDL and HGV licence classes explained

Licence classes depend on the size and weight of the vehicle and whether you tow a trailer. Pick the class that matches the work you want and the jobs near you.

United StatesClass B CDLClass A CDL
United KingdomC1 or CC+E
CanadaVaries by provinceClass 1 (AZ in Ontario)
AustraliaMR or HRHC or MC
New ZealandClass 2 or 4Class 3 or 5

In the US you usually need to be 21 to drive across state lines, though many states allow 18 for driving within the state. Endorsements cover special work such as hazardous materials (which needs a background check), tankers and double or triple trailers. In the UK, you need a full car licence first, and professional drivers also need the Driver Certificate of Professional Competence (Driver CPC), with 35 hours of periodic training every five years.

In Australia you usually work up through the classes and must hold a lower class for a set time before upgrading, often at least a year before heavy combination. Canada and New Zealand use staged learner and full licences, and many Canadian provinces require mandatory entry-level training before the road test.

The truck driver medical exam

Every country covered here requires a medical for heavy vehicle drivers, repeated over time. In the US it is done by a certified medical examiner, and the certificate is usually valid for up to 24 months, sometimes less. In the UK a doctor completes a medical report when you apply for lorry entitlement, renewed periodically and more often from age 45.

Expect a health questionnaire, vision and hearing checks, blood pressure, a urine test and a physical examination. Conditions that often need extra assessment include diabetes treated with insulin, seizures, heart conditions, high blood pressure, vision problems and sleep apnea.

Be completely honest on medical forms, and tell your examiner about every medication. Talk to your doctor before the exam if you have a long-term condition, and see a doctor if you snore heavily, wake up choking or feel sleepy during the day. Untreated sleep apnea is a serious fatigue risk, and it is treatable. This guide is general information, not medical or legal advice.

Drug and alcohol testing is standard. In the US, employers also check a national drug and alcohol clearinghouse before hiring. Refusing a test is usually treated the same as failing it.

How to choose a truck driving school

The main routes are private truck driving schools, community or technical colleges, company-sponsored training and, in some places, apprenticeships. In the US, federal entry-level driver training must be completed with a provider on the official training provider registry before your first skills test for a class A or B licence or certain endorsements.

Questions to ask any school

  • How many hours behind the wheel will I personally get, and how many students share each truck?
  • What vehicle will I test in, and will my licence carry any restrictions?
  • What does the total cost include, and what costs extra, such as retests and medicals?
  • Are you on the official provider registry or approved list where required?
  • Can I speak to recent graduates?

With company-sponsored training, read the repayment clause before signing. Find out how long you must stay, what you owe if you leave early and whether you owe anything if you fail the test. Walk away from any school that guarantees a job or pay, or pressures you to sign that day.

How to pass your CDL or HGV tests

Testing usually comes in three parts: knowledge or theory tests, a vehicle inspection or safety demonstration, and practical driving including basic control skills and a road test. The UK adds a hazard perception test and case studies. In the US, you must hold your commercial learner's permit for at least 14 days before the skills test.

  • Study the official handbook in short daily sessions, not just practice questions.
  • Learn a fixed inspection routine and say out loud what you are checking and why.
  • Back up slowly with small steering inputs, and get out and look whenever you are unsure.
  • On the road test, check mirrors often, signal early, slow down before bends and take turns wide without crossing into oncoming traffic.
  • Run a full mock test day with your instructor before the real one.

The night before, lay out your documents, sleep properly and eat breakfast. If you make a small mistake during the test, let it go and drive the next part well.

Hours of service and rest rules for new drivers

Driving hours rules exist because fatigue causes crashes. Electronic logging devices or digital tachographs record your time, and these simplified examples show the general shape. Check the current official rules where you drive, since exceptions apply.

  • US property-carrying drivers: up to 11 hours driving after 10 consecutive hours off, no driving beyond the 14th hour after coming on duty, a 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving, and weekly limits of 60 hours in 7 days or 70 hours in 8 days.
  • UK drivers' hours: up to 9 hours driving a day (10 hours twice a week), a 45-minute break after 4.5 hours of driving, a maximum of 56 hours in a week and 90 hours in two weeks, and daily rest of normally 11 hours.
  • Canada (federal, south of 60): up to 13 hours driving and 14 hours on duty a day, with at least 10 hours off.
  • Australia (standard hours for solo drivers in most states): no more than 12 hours work in 24, at least 7 continuous hours stationary rest and 15 minutes rest in any 5.5 hours of work.

Treat these as limits, not targets. If you feel drowsy, stop somewhere safe and rest. Caffeine is not a substitute for sleep.

How to land your first trucking job

Employers usually look for the right licence class and endorsements, a current medical, a clean or reasonably clean driving record, a clear drug and alcohol history, recognized training and a steady work history. Your driving record in your own car counts, so protect it.

Questions to ask recruiters

  • How is pay calculated, and is there pay for waiting time or extra stops?
  • How long will I drive with a trainer or mentor?
  • How often do drivers actually get home?
  • What freight will I haul, and in what areas?
  • Are there any training contracts or repayment clauses?
  • Can I talk to some current drivers?

Red flags

  • Very high pay promises with no clear explanation of how it is calculated.
  • Pressure to accept on the spot, or refusal to put the offer in writing.
  • Any request to break hours rules or skip inspections.
  • Lease-purchase truck deals offered to brand new drivers without clear terms.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to become a truck driver?

It varies with your country, training route and test availability. Some full-time training programs run for a few weeks, while college programs and apprenticeships take longer. Add time for the medical, learner stage, any minimum holding period such as 14 days with a US learner's permit, and test bookings. Most people should plan for at least a few months from first research to first paid job.

How old do you have to be to drive a truck?

In the US you usually need to be 21 to drive across state lines, while many states allow 18 for driving within the state. In the UK the minimum is generally 18 with the Driver CPC. In Canada, Australia and New Zealand it depends on the licence class and how long you have held lower licences. Always check your local licensing agency.

Is company-sponsored CDL training really free?

It can mean low or no upfront cost, but there is usually a catch: you agree to work for that company for a set time. If you leave early, you may have to repay some or all of the training cost. Before signing, get the contract in writing and check how long you must stay, what you would owe and whether the amount reduces over time.

Can you become a truck driver with diabetes or sleep apnea?

Often yes, but these conditions usually need extra assessment, and rules differ by country. Diabetes treated with insulin and sleep apnea are commonly reviewed, and you may need reports from your doctor or proof that treatment is working. Talk to your doctor early, be honest on every medical form and check your licensing agency's current medical standards before paying for training.

What is the difference between a CDL and an HGV licence?

They are the US and UK names for similar things. A commercial driver's license (CDL) in the US comes in classes A, B and C. In the UK, heavy goods vehicle (HGV), also called large goods vehicle (LGV), entitlement comes in categories C1, C and C+E, and professional drivers also need the Driver CPC. Each system has its own tests and medical rules.

What is the best trucking job for a new driver?

There is no single best one. Long-haul and regional dry van or refrigerated work are the most common starting points, because many employers train new drivers for them. Local delivery jobs get you home daily but may want more experience and involve more physical work. Choose based on time away from home your family can manage, the jobs near you and the training offered.

Your first step today: get a copy of your driving record and look up the official licence requirements for the class you want where you live. With those two things in front of you, you will know exactly what stands between you and your first day of training.
