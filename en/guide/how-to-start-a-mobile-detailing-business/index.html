How to Start a Mobile Detailing Business: A Beginner's Plan

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you love a clean car and want to work for yourself, mobile detailing (valeting in the UK) is one of the more straightforward businesses to start. To start a mobile detailing business, you build a short menu of three or four clear packages, buy only the starter kit those packages need, register the business, get public liability and customer vehicle insurance, plan how you will contain and dispose of wash water, then price every job from your real time and costs by vehicle size.

The hard parts are not the washing. They are underquoting, skipping the right insurance and letting suds run into a storm drain. This guide walks you through each step in order, with tables and checklists you can use this week. It is general information, so check local rules and get advice on tax and insurance for your own situation.

What mobile detailing really involves

Detailing is not a quick car wash. It means cleaning, correcting and protecting a car carefully, inside and out, at the customer's home or workplace. Your customers are busy families, commuters, pet owners, people selling cars, and small businesses with vans or company cars.

A typical day means loading the van early, two to four jobs depending on size and condition, driving between them and restocking in the evening. The work is physical. Expect sore knees, wet weeks and cold mornings, and plan to look after your back, hands and skin from the start.

  • Pros: low startup cost compared with a fixed unit, flexible hours, loyal repeat customers and visible results
  • Cons: weather, seasonal dips, travel time and the risk of damaging a customer's car if you rush

Before you spend money, spend a day shadowing a working detailer or detail a few friends' cars and time every stage honestly.

Choose services and packages customers understand

Customers want to know what you will do, how long it takes and how the car will look afterward. Three or four plainly named packages answer that better than a long list of technical terms.

PackageWhat it coversGood for
Maintenance washSafe wash, wheels, glass, quick vacuumRegular clients
Exterior detailWash, decontamination, trims, protectionShine and protection
Interior detailFull vacuum, seats, stains, dash, glassFamilies and pet owners
Full detailExterior and interior togetherFirst visits and sales

Under each package, list exactly what is included and add one line saying what is not, such as heavy pet hair, mold, deep stains or paint correction. Sell those as add-ons: pet hair removal, odor treatment, headlight restoration, engine bay cleaning and light paint correction.

Hold back the risky work. Machine paint correction beyond a light polish, ceramic coatings and wet sanding can damage a car badly. Practice on scrap panels for months and refer these jobs to a specialist until you are confident.

Mobile car detailing equipment list

Start with what your menu needs and upgrade only when a real problem appears, such as jobs taking too long.

ItemStarterUpgrade later
WashingBuckets, grit guards, mitts, sprayerPressure washer and foam lance
WaterRinseless products, small tankLarger tank with pump and hose reel
VacuumWet and dry vacuumExtractor, cordless vacuum
PolishingHand applicatorsDual-action polisher and pads
LightingHandheld LED lightStand light, color-matched lamps

Add plenty of good microfiber towels, separate brushes and buckets for wheels, a containment mat, interior brushes and protective gear. Buy secondhand vacuums and polishers only after testing them, and never buy unlabeled chemicals or damaged safety gear.

Avoid heavy-duty patio pressure washers. Very high pressure can lift loose paint, damage trims and badges and force water past seals.

Register the business and get the right insurance

Register with your tax authority, choose a business name you have checked is free, open a separate bank account and ask your local council or city about licenses for mobile trading. Keep every receipt and log your business mileage from day one.

Type of coverWhat it is for
Public liability (general liability)Injury to people or damage to property caused by your work
Customer vehicle coverDamage to customers' cars while in your care
Business vehicle insuranceYour van used for business, not just personal use
Tools and equipment coverTheft or damage to your kit
Employer's liability or workers' compensationOften required once you take on staff

Customer vehicle cover is the one beginners miss. Often called care, custody and control, it covers damage to a customer's car while you work on it. Policies vary a lot, and some exclude paint correction, chemical damage or high-value vehicles, so read yours carefully and describe exactly what you do to your broker.

Handle water runoff and chemicals safely

Most street and car park (parking lot) drains are storm drains that flow straight into rivers and the sea without treatment. Wash water carries soap, oil, brake dust and grime, and in many places letting it enter storm drains is illegal or restricted.

  1. Ask your council or water authority whether runoff may enter storm drains, whether you need a permit, and where you may dispose of collected water.
  2. Use a containment mat under the car and keep spraying inside its edges.
  3. Recover water with a wet vacuum or pump into a clearly labeled waste tank.
  4. Dispose of it only as the authority allows, often into a foul (sanitary) sewer, never a storm drain.
  5. Use rinseless or waterless methods for light to moderate dirt and during hosepipe bans.

Biodegradable does not mean safe to pour down a drain. Always ask before using a customer's water or power, and check site rules at apartments and workplaces.

Never mix chemicals. Read every label and safety data sheet, dilute exactly as directed, wear gloves and eye protection, take extra care with wheel cleaners and keep chemicals upright and secured in the van. If a product gets in your eyes or you feel unwell after breathing fumes, follow the first aid advice on the label and get medical help.

The exterior and interior detail in order

A consistent order makes your work faster and protects the paint. Start every job with a walk-around inspection and photos of existing damage, and agree them with the customer.

Exterior

  1. Wheels and tires first, with separate tools
  2. Pre-rinse and pre-wash to loosen grit
  3. Two-bucket hand wash, top down, rinsing the mitt often
  4. Dry with clean, soft microfiber
  5. Decontaminate paint if the package includes it
  6. Glass, trims and tire dressing
  7. Apply protection, then a final walk-around

Interior

  1. Remove personal items carefully into a box for the customer
  2. Dry vacuum from top to bottom, including the trunk (boot)
  3. Clean floor mats
  4. Treat seats, fabric or leather, and light stains
  5. Wipe the dash, controls, vents and screens with suitable products
  6. Clean inside glass and door shuts (door jambs)

Work in shade where you can, because hot sun dries products onto the paint and leaves spots.

How much to charge for car detailing

Prices vary so much by town that any figure here would be wrong somewhere. Use a method instead. Build a table of each package against size bands: small cars, medium cars and estates (station wagons), and large vehicles such as SUVs, pickup trucks (utes) and seven-seaters.

For each package and size, add up:

  • Your time on the car, plus setup, pack-down and travel
  • Products, towels and pads used
  • Fuel and van wear
  • A share of yearly overheads: insurance, phone, software, equipment replacement, van servicing, advertising and accounting
  • Tax and a margin for quiet weeks

Time real jobs for the first few months and price on how long you actually take, not how long you hope to take. Then compare with local competitors and compete on quality and reliability, never on being cheapest.

Always ask about condition before quoting. Ask about pet hair, stains, smells and the date of the last clean, and request photos. If the car is far worse than described, explain politely before you start and agree any extra charge. Set a travel or minimum call-out charge and take deposits for full details.

Find clients and turn them into regulars

Start with people you know, then add a simple website or social media page with clear packages, your service area and before and after photos taken with the customer's permission. Approach local businesses with vans or company cars, car dealers and car clubs.

Your van and your manners are marketing too: arrive on time, keep the driveway tidy and leave no mess behind. Ask happy customers for a review the same day.

Repeat work is where the business becomes steady. After a full detail, offer a maintenance plan with a wash every two to four weeks at a slightly better price per visit, because a car that never gets filthy is quicker to clean. Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a mobile detailing business?

Startup costs depend on whether you already have a suitable vehicle. Many people begin with buckets, grit guards, microfiber, a wet and dry vacuum, rinseless products, a small water tank, a containment mat, safety gear and insurance. Pressure washers, polishers and larger tanks can come later. Keep the buy-now list short and upgrade only when a real problem appears.

Do I need a license to do mobile car detailing?

It depends on where you live. Most places expect you to register with the tax authority, and some cities or councils require a business license or permit for mobile trading or mobile vehicle cleaning. Water and runoff rules can also apply. Ask your local council or city and your water authority, and keep a written record of their answers.

What insurance does a mobile detailer need?

Usually public liability (general liability) for injury or property damage, customer vehicle cover for damage to cars in your care, business use insurance for your van and cover for tools and equipment. If you hire staff, employer's liability or workers' compensation is often required. Read exclusions carefully, especially for paint correction, chemicals and high-value cars.

Can you do mobile detailing without water access?

Yes, in many cases. Carry your own water in a tank, or use rinseless and waterless methods, which suit cars with light to moderate dirt. They are not suitable for heavy mud, salt or sand, because wiping grit across paint can scratch it. Use plenty of clean microfiber towels, turn them often and never press hard.

How long does a full car detail take?

It varies with vehicle size, condition and your experience. A full detail on a large, dirty vehicle can take most of a day, while a tidy small car is far quicker. Pet hair can double interior time. Time your own jobs by package, size and condition for the first few months so your bookings and prices match reality.

Is a mobile detailing business profitable?

It can be, but nobody can promise a figure. Profit depends on your prices, costs, travel, local demand and how many jobs you can do safely each week. Detailers who price from real timings, charge for add-ons and travel, avoid being the cheapest and build regular maintenance clients tend to have steadier income than those relying on one-off jobs.

Your first step today is to write a one-page menu with three or four plainly named packages, a line saying what each does not include and a short list of add-ons. Then contact your local water authority this week and ask exactly how you may dispose of wash water.
