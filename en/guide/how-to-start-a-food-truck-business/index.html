How to Start a Food Truck Business: Permits, Safety and Pricing

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If people keep telling you that your cooking deserves a queue, a food truck or market stall can feel like the natural next step. To start a food truck business, test your food small first at a market or pop-up, check every permit layer before you buy a vehicle, set up a food safety system that passes inspection, cost each menu item properly, and then build a calendar of locations and events.

This guide follows that order, because the most expensive mistakes in mobile food happen when people buy a truck and paint it before they know the rules. You will find a permit checklist, safe temperature targets and a worked menu costing example.

Is a food truck business right for you

The reality is long days, heat in a small kitchen, early starts for prep, cleaning after every service and slow days when the weather turns. There is also plenty of admin: permits, logs, supplier orders and event applications. If you still feel excited after reading that, keep going.

Test the idea small first

The cheapest way to learn whether people will pay for your food is to sell some of it, legally, at small scale. A weekend market stall, a pop-up at a friendly venue or catering for a friend's event lets you test three things:

  • Whether strangers, not just friends, like the food and come back
  • Whether you can serve it fast enough during a rush
  • Whether the numbers work at a price people will pay

Have an honest family and money conversation before committing savings or borrowing. Agree in advance what success looks like and how long you will give it.

Choose a food truck concept people queue for

What you love to cook and what sells are not always the same, so look for the overlap. Walk your local markets, festivals and lunch spots and note what is missing.

  • Keep the menu short. A focused menu of a few items means faster service, less waste and simpler stock.
  • Choose food that holds well and can be served in a few minutes
  • Prefer dishes that are easy to eat standing up, from a box or by hand
  • Pick a name and simple branding that say clearly what you sell

Write a one-sentence concept, such as slow-cooked beef sandwiches with house pickles, served fast. If you cannot describe it in one sentence, your customers will struggle to remember it.

Food truck, trailer, cart or market stall

Mobile food comes in four main shapes, each with trade-offs.

TypeStrengthsWatch out for
Food truck (catering or food van)Self-contained, quick to move between sitesHigher cost, engine and vehicle repairs stop trading
Concession (catering) trailerMore kitchen space for the moneyNeeds a towing vehicle and the right licence category
Food cartLow cost, small footprintLimited menu and storage
Market stall (pitch) with canopy or gazeboCheapest way to start and testSetup time, weather, needs weights and tie-downs

Before buying a used unit, check gas certification, electrics, the water system, rust and ventilation, and ask your health department or council what their plan review requires. Leasing or renting a unit first is a sensible way to learn what layout you need. Tape out the kitchen on a floor at home and walk through a rush to test your workflow.

Food truck permits and licenses

Permits vary widely by country, state or province and city, so check every layer before trading and before buying a vehicle. This is general information, not legal advice.

  • United States. Usually a business license, a mobile food vendor or health permit from the local health department (often after a plan review and a pre-opening inspection), a fire inspection for cooking and propane systems, a seller's permit for sales tax, and separate permits for each city or county where you trade. Many cities limit where and when trucks can park.
  • United Kingdom. Register as a food business with the council where the vehicle is usually kept at least 28 days before trading (registration is free), and check for a street trading licence or consent from each council plus market or event permission.
  • Canada: a provincial health permit plus a municipal vending licence
  • Australia: register or notify with your council or state food authority, and check food safety supervisor rules and mobile vending permits
  • New Zealand: operate under a food control plan or national programme, and check council trading permits

Many US areas also require an approved commissary for prep, storage, water and waste, and almost everywhere you may only prepare food in registered premises. Home kitchens are usually not allowed unless separately registered.

Food safety rules for food trucks

A written food safety system based on HACCP principles is what gets you through inspections. In plain words: find the hazards, decide the points where you control them, set limits, monitor, fix problems, check it works and keep records.

ControlTarget to follow (check your local code)
Cold holding40 to 41°F (4 to 5°C) or below in the US; 8°C or below by law in the UK, 5°C recommended
Hot holding135°F (57°C) or above in the US; 63°C in the UK; 60°C in Australia and New Zealand
PoultryCook to 165°F (74°C)
Ground meatCook to 160°F (71°C)
Reheating for hot holdingQuickly to 165°F (74°C), steaming hot, once only

Use a calibrated probe thermometer and keep temperature logs. Separate raw and ready-to-eat food with color-coded boards, and sanitize at the right strength using test strips. You need a dedicated handwash sink with hot and cold running water, soap and paper towels, separate from food prep and dishwashing sinks.

Staff with vomiting, diarrhea, fever with sore throat, jaundice or infected cuts must not handle food; a common rule is to stay off until 48 hours after symptoms stop. Keep an allergen matrix for every menu item, train staff never to guess, and call emergency services immediately if a customer shows signs of a serious allergic reaction.

Check training rules too: many US areas require food handler cards and a certified food protection manager, and in the UK food handlers must be trained or supervised in food hygiene suited to their role.

Gas, fire and equipment safety

Fires and gas leaks are the biggest physical risks in a mobile kitchen. Build safety into the setup from day one.

  • Have propane (LPG) systems installed and certified by a qualified gas fitter; in the UK, a registered gas engineer qualified for LPG in mobile catering
  • Secure cylinders upright, outside or in a vented locker, and check for leaks with soapy water, never a flame
  • Carry a fire extinguisher for cooking oil fires (class K in the US, class F or wet chemical in the UK, Australia and New Zealand), a general purpose extinguisher and a fire blanket
  • Never use water on a fat fire
  • Fit a carbon monoxide alarm inside and run generators outside, away from openings
  • Weight and tie down canopies and gazebos so wind cannot lift them

Many places require a hood with a fire suppression system over fryers and grills. Ask your fire department or local authority what applies before you finalize your layout.

How to price a food truck menu

Cost every portion from its recipe, including garnish, sauce, oil and packaging. Divide the price you paid for each ingredient by the amount you bought, then multiply by the amount in one portion. Allow for yield, since meat and vegetables lose weight when trimmed and cooked.

Then set a starting price by dividing the portion cost by a target food cost percentage you choose after researching your market. Check that price against nearby vendors and what customers will pay.

Example only, made-up round numbersAmount
Portion cost of a beef sandwich, including box and napkin3.00
Starting price at a 30 percent target (3.00 divided by 0.30)10.00
Minus card fee, waste allowance and event commission share1.00
Contribution left per sandwich6.00

In this example, the 6.00 contribution is what pays fixed costs such as permits, commissary rent, insurance and fuel. If those were 600 a week, the vendor would need to sell 100 sandwiches a week just to cover them. Run this sum for your own menu before you sign for any vehicle.

Finding food truck locations and events

Where you park matters as much as what you cook. Mix several kinds of trading so one cancelled event does not empty your week.

  • Legal street spots, checking parking and trading rules for each
  • Office parks, breweries and private sites where the owner agrees and rules allow
  • Farmers markets and craft fairs, which are great for testing
  • Festivals and big events, which bring crowds but also fees, commissions and detailed applications
  • Private catering and weddings, booked ahead with a deposit

Events often ask for proof of insurance, permits and your food hygiene rating, so keep copies ready. Track every spot you trade: date, weather, sales and costs. After a few months, the numbers will show which locations deserve a regular place in your calendar.

Insurance, money and marketing basics

Insurance usually includes general liability (public liability) and product liability, commercial vehicle insurance covering business use, and equipment and stock cover. In the UK, employer's liability insurance is a legal requirement once you employ staff, and workers' compensation rules apply in the US, Canada and Australia.

Keep business money in a separate account, record daily sales and costs, and set tax money aside. Sales tax, VAT and GST rules vary; in the UK, hot takeaway food is usually standard-rated for VAT if you are registered. Ask an accountant early.

Frequently asked questions

What permits do I need for a food truck?

It depends on your country and city. In the US, expect a business license, a health permit after a plan review and inspection, a fire inspection, a seller's permit and separate permits for each city you trade in. In the UK, register with your council at least 28 days before trading and check street trading licences for each area.

Can I prepare food truck food at home?

Usually not. Most places require food for mobile trading to be prepared in an approved commissary or registered commercial kitchen, which also handles storage, water filling and waste. Home kitchens are only allowed where they are separately registered or covered by specific home food rules. Check with your health department or council before planning your prep.

Is a food trailer better than a food truck?

A concession trailer often gives more kitchen space for the money, and if the towing vehicle breaks down you can swap it. A food truck is self-contained and quicker to move between sites, but an engine problem stops trading. Trailers need a suitable towing vehicle and possibly a specific licence category, so check local rules first.

How do I price food truck menu items?

Cost every portion from its recipe, including sauces, garnish and packaging. Divide that cost by a target food cost percentage you choose after researching your market to get a starting price, then compare it with nearby vendors. Subtract card fees, waste and event commission to see what each item contributes toward fixed costs and profit.

How do I start a food stall at a market?

Register as a food business with your local authority, check that you prepare food in registered premises, get public liability insurance and complete suitable food hygiene training. Then apply to the market, which will usually ask for proof of insurance, registration and your hygiene rating. Start with a short menu and a canopy that is properly weighted.

What is the most important safety rule for a food truck?

Temperature control comes first: keep cold food cold, hot food hot, cook to safe temperatures and check everything with a calibrated probe thermometer and written logs. Close behind are handwashing at a dedicated sink, keeping ill staff away from food, honest allergen information and certified gas installations with the right fire extinguishers.

Before you browse a single truck listing, take one step today: call your local health department or council and ask what a mobile food vendor needs in your area, then write each permit on a list with who issues it. That list, plus one costed menu item, tells you more about your future business than any paint job ever will.
