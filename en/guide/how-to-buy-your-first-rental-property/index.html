How to Buy Your First Rental Property: 8 Steps and the Real Math

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

You have some savings, you keep seeing listings with tempting yields, and you want to know whether being a landlord is a smart move or an expensive mistake. To buy your first rental property safely, get two separate cash cushions in place, analyze every cost line until you know the real monthly cash flow, stress test the mortgage at higher rates, and only then make an offer on a property tenants actually want.

This guide walks you through those steps with worked numbers you can copy onto your own deal. It is general education, not financial, tax or legal advice: landlord rules vary by country, state and city, so check local rules and speak to a qualified mortgage broker, accountant and lawyer (solicitor) before you buy.

How a rental property makes and loses money

A rental can pay you in four ways: monthly cash flow (rent left after every cost and the mortgage), loan paydown as the tenant's rent reduces your debt, appreciation if the property rises in value, and tax effects that differ by country. None of them is guaranteed.

It can also lose money in very ordinary ways: empty months, a boiler (furnace) that dies, a tenant who stops paying, an interest rate rise when your fixed deal ends, or falling prices in the area. Borrowing magnifies losses as well as gains, and if you are forced to sell in a weak market you can lose more than your deposit.

There is also the 2 a.m. part nobody advertises. A rental is a small business with a customer who can call you about a leak on a holiday weekend. If that sounds unbearable, budget for a property manager from the start.

Get your own finances ready first

Before you view a single property, build two safety nets and keep them separate:

  • Personal emergency fund. Three to six months of your household's essential costs, so a job loss or a car repair never forces you to raid the rental's money.
  • Property reserve. Three to six months of the property's full costs, including the mortgage. For a house with 736 of monthly operating costs and an 853 mortgage, six months is 9,534.

Then check your credit reports, dispute any errors in writing and avoid opening new credit in the months before applying. Paying off high-interest debt first is often the best return available: clearing a card at around 20 percent is a guaranteed saving with no tenants involved.

Lenders will look at your credit history, stable income, existing debts, the source of your deposit, the expected rent and the property itself. Gather payslips or accounts, tax returns and bank statements now so you can move quickly.

How much money you need to buy a rental

Rental mortgages, called buy-to-let mortgages in the UK and investment property loans in the US, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, usually need a deposit (down payment) of 20 to 25 percent or more. The deposit is only part of the cash. Here is an illustrative house priced at 180,000 (the math works in any currency):

Cash neededAmount
Deposit, 25 percent45,000
Closing (completion) costs5,400
Initial repairs4,600
Total cash invested55,000
Property reserve, six months9,534
Total before buying64,534

That is on top of your untouched personal emergency fund. Closing costs include legal or conveyancing fees, lender and valuation (appraisal) fees, a survey or inspection, and transfer taxes such as stamp duty, which can be much higher in some places and may carry a surcharge for investment properties. Ask your lawyer or conveyancer for a written estimate before you offer.

Choose a location and property tenants want

Buy where people reliably want to rent, not where prices merely look cheap. Things to check in any neighborhood:

  • Jobs and the local economy, ideally more than one big employer.
  • Transport links and commute times to where people work.
  • Schools, if you are targeting families.
  • Crime trends over several years, not one headline.
  • Achieved rents and how long listings stay empty, which local letting agents can tell you.
  • Local landlord rules, licensing schemes and any limits on short-term holiday lets.

For a first purchase, a modest house or flat in a steady area is usually easier to manage than a shared house. Flats and condos come with service charges or HOA fees that can rise, and leasehold flats may add ground rent and lease-length issues, so read the building accounts and lease before you fall in love. Houses in multiple occupation often need a licence and more management.

How to calculate cash flow on a rental property

Start with a realistic rent, using the low end of what local agents say similar homes actually achieve, then subtract every line:

  • Vacancy, about 5 to 8 percent of rent.
  • Repairs and maintenance, about 5 to 10 percent.
  • A capital reserve for roofs, boilers and appliances, about 5 to 10 percent.
  • Property management, often about 8 to 12 percent, plus tenant-finding fees.
  • Property tax (in the UK, council tax is usually paid by the tenant while let), landlord insurance, service charges or HOA fees, ground rent and any utilities you pay.

Rent minus those operating costs is net operating income (NOI). NOI minus the mortgage is cash flow. Cap rate is annual NOI divided by price; cash-on-cash return is annual cash flow divided by total cash invested. Gross yield (annual rent divided by price) is a quick headline that ignores every cost.

MonthlyHouse AFlat C

Operating costs7361,527
NOI1,064273
Mortgage853711

Gross yield12.0%14.4%

Flat C, priced at 150,000 in a town with weak demand, has the higher yield and loses 438 a month once high vacancy, an old building's repairs, a 350 service charge, ground rent and landlord-paid utilities arrive. House A, at 180,000 and fully managed, earns 211 a month, a 4.6 percent cash-on-cash return. It is not thrilling, and that is what a sensible deal often looks like.

The 1 percent rule (monthly rent at least 1 percent of price) and the 50 percent rule (costs are half the rent) are only screening shortcuts. Flat C passes the 1 percent rule and still loses money. Always work through to NOI and cash flow.

Stress test the mortgage before you sign

Rates move, and fixed deals end. Run your deal at your offered rate, then at 2 and 3 percentage points higher. Here is House A on the same 135,000 loan:

5.0%725339
6.5%853211
8.0%99173
9.5%1,135-71

Then cut the rent by 10 percent at the highest rate, because markets can soften just as borrowing costs rise. If you could not carry the worst result for a year or two without selling, you need a bigger deposit, a cheaper property or a different deal.

Lenders run their own tests too, comparing rent with a stressed payment (often called an interest cover ratio in the UK or a debt service coverage ratio in the US). Passing their test does not make the deal safe for you. Interest-only loans lift cash flow but leave the whole loan to repay later, so they need a credible exit plan. Speak to a mortgage broker early; an agreement in principle (pre-approval) also shows sellers you are serious.

Make an offer and buy without nasty surprises

  1. View like a landlord, not a home buyer: check the roof, damp, boiler or furnace age, wiring, windows and how hard-wearing the finishes are.
  2. Get an independent survey or home inspection and use it to adjust your numbers.
  3. Offer only what your worksheet supports at a stressed rate, and renegotiate or walk away if the inspection reveals costly problems.
  4. Let your lawyer or conveyancer handle searches, title and contracts.
  5. Arrange landlord insurance, not a standard homeowner (buildings and contents) policy, before you own the keys.
  6. Plan the safety work and repairs needed before the first tenant moves in.

Use several written quotes from tradespeople for any work. Get repairs into three groups: must fix, should fix and nice to have. Spend first on safety and anything that would put a good tenant off.

Landlord safety rules and fair tenant screening

Legal duties vary widely, so check current local rules. Common examples include an annual gas safety check, electrical inspections at least every five years in England, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, energy performance certificates and Right to Rent checks in England; healthy homes standards in New Zealand; and habitability standards plus lead paint disclosure for US homes built before 1978.

Write your tenant screening criteria down, such as income relative to rent, references and lawful credit checks with consent, and apply them to every applicant the same way. Fair housing and anti-discrimination laws (the Fair Housing Act in the US, the Equality Act 2010 in the UK and similar laws elsewhere) forbid treating people differently for protected characteristics.

Deposits have rules too. In England and Wales, deposits for assured shorthold tenancies must be protected in a government-approved scheme within 30 days, while Australia and New Zealand lodge bonds with an official authority. Never attempt a self-help eviction by changing locks, removing belongings or cutting utilities; it is illegal in most places. Always follow the legal process.

Is buying a rental property worth it

It can be, for people who buy with real reserves, honest numbers and a long time horizon. It is rarely the passive income it is sold as. A good first deal often makes a modest monthly profit, slowly pays down its loan and asks you for some time every month.

Before you commit, write your readiness number: six months of your personal essentials, plus the full cash a typical local rental needs, plus six months of its costs. Compare it with what you have saved today. Frequently asked questions

How much deposit do I need for a rental property?

Rental and buy-to-let lenders commonly ask for 20 to 25 percent of the price, sometimes more for flats, unusual properties or first-time landlords. On top of that you need closing costs, money for initial repairs and a property reserve of three to six months of costs. A bigger deposit lowers your payment and helps the deal survive rate rises, but never drain your emergency fund to reach it.

What is a good cash flow on a rental property?

A good cash flow is one that stays positive after every realistic cost and still survives a rate 2 to 3 points higher. There is no universal figure. In the worked example above, 211 a month on 55,000 invested, a 4.6 percent cash-on-cash return, is respectable because it already includes management, vacancy and repair reserves. A bigger number that ignores those costs is not real.

Is the 1 percent rule still valid?

Only as a rough filter. The 1 percent rule says monthly rent should be at least 1 percent of the price, but cheap properties often pass because they are cheap for bad reasons, and in expensive areas almost nothing passes. A flat can pass the rule and still lose money once service charges, vacancy and repairs are counted. Use it to decide which listings deserve a full analysis.

Should I use a property manager for my first rental?

It depends on your time, distance and temperament. Managers often charge about 8 to 12 percent of rent plus tenant-finding fees, and in return handle repairs, rent collection, inspections and legal paperwork. Even if you self-manage, run your numbers with a management fee included, because you may need one later. If a deal only works without a manager, its margin is thin.

Can I use my home equity to buy a rental property?

Often you can, through a home equity line of credit in the US or Canada, a remortgage or further advance in the UK, or a top-up in Australia and New Zealand. The risk is that the rental becomes effectively fully borrowed and your family home secures part of it. Stress test both properties together and make sure you could pay both if the rental stood empty for months.

Do I pay tax on rental income?

Usually, yes. Rental income is generally taxable, and many expenses such as repairs, insurance and agent fees can often be deducted, but rules on mortgage interest, depreciation and capital gains differ by country and change often. Open a separate bank account for the property, keep every receipt and speak to a qualified accountant before you buy so you know how your deal will be taxed.

Your first step today is to pick one real listing in an area you know and fill in every cost line, from vacancy to insurance, then rerun the cash flow at a rate 3 points higher. If it still makes money and you could fund the deposit, costs and reserve without touching your emergency fund, you are ready to talk to a mortgage broker and an accountant about it.
