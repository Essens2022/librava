How to Start a Dog Walking Business: A Safe Step by Step Guide

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you love dogs and want flexible work you can fit around school hours, another job or retirement, walking dogs for neighbors can grow into a real business. To start a dog walking business, check your local rules, get specialist pet care insurance, decide on a short list of services, build prices from your real time and costs, and win your first clients through meet-and-greets with people nearby.

This guide covers each step, plus the safety and trust habits that keep clients coming back: secure key handling, written permissions, and a plan for emergencies. Most of it applies to drop-in visits and pet sitting too.

Is dog walking work right for you

The honest daily reality includes early mornings, rainy walks, muddy towels and a lot of driving or walking between homes. Holidays are your busiest time, exactly when friends and family are relaxing. You will also hold other people's keys and be responsible for animals they love like family.

Temperament matters as much as love of animals. You need patience, calm under pressure, good timekeeping and the confidence to say no to a dog you cannot handle safely.

Ways to build experience first

  • Care for friends' and relatives' pets and keep notes on what you learn
  • Volunteer to walk dogs at a local shelter or rescue
  • Take a pet first aid course
  • Learn dog body language before you take on strangers' dogs

Starting as a side business lets you test the work, learn your real timings and build reviews before relying on it for income.

Choose your pet care services

A short menu works better than offering everything. Pick services that fit your skills, your hours and your area.

Avoid home boarding and dog day care as a beginner. In England both need a council license, and in many other places they bring extra rules and much more risk.

Then draw your service area. A tight zone around home keeps travel short, which matters more to your pricing than most people expect.

Do you need a license to be a dog walker

Many places do not license simple dog walking or drop-in visits, but some do. Certain cities, parks and councils require a permit for commercial dog walkers or limit how many dogs one person may walk at once. Check before your first paying walk.

Your pre-launch checklist

  1. Register your business as your country requires and choose a name.
  2. Ask your city, county or council about permits, park rules and dog limits.
  3. Get specialist pet care business insurance.
  4. Set up records for every payment and expense, and ask an accountant about self-employment taxes.
  5. Prepare a service agreement, a client information form and a vet release form.

Specialist pet care insurance typically includes liability cover (public liability in the UK) and cover for animals in your care, sometimes called care, custody and control. Read what is excluded, such as lost keys, vet bills, dog bites or damage in the home. In the US and Canada, a bond (often called a fidelity or dishonesty bond) protects clients against theft and is different from insurance. If you drive pets, check your vehicle policy covers business use. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Dog walking gear and safety kit

Your kit protects the dogs and you. Pack it the night before so early starts run smoothly.

  • Strong leads (leashes), spare flat collars and a spare harness in common sizes
  • A second lead or double clip for escape-prone dogs
  • Plenty of waste bags, plus a treat pouch with treats the owner has approved
  • Water and a collapsible bowl
  • Towels for muddy paws and a reflective vest or light for dark mornings
  • A pet first aid kit and a charged phone (cell phone or mobile)

Do not use retractable leads on client dogs. Check harness fit, collars, ID tags and clips at every pickup, because a slipped harness near a road is how most lost dog stories start. In the UK, an ID tag on the collar is a legal requirement in public and microchipping dogs is compulsory.

Never let a client dog off lead without the owner's written permission and a proven recall, and never in unfenced areas near roads. Report any incident, however small, to the owner the same day.

How many dogs can you walk at once

Start with one dog or one household at a time. That is the safest way to learn each dog's habits and to build your own handling skills.

Where local rules set a maximum for group walks, it is often somewhere around four to six dogs. Whatever the rules say, never take more dogs than you can physically control and pick up after at the same time.

Before any group walk

  • Get each owner's written consent to walk their dog with others
  • Introduce dogs gradually, on neutral ground
  • Leave out reactive or unneutered dogs, unvaccinated dogs and puppies whose vaccinations are not complete
  • Check with the owner and their vet before including dogs with health issues

Learn the early warning signs in dog body language: a stiff body, whale eye (showing the whites of the eyes), lip licking and growling. Calmly create distance when you see them, and never use punishment or harsh corrections.

Keeping pets safe in emergencies

Collect vaccination records, the vet's details and a signed vet release form before the first visit. The release form authorizes treatment if the owner cannot be reached and states a spending limit. Know where the nearest emergency (out of hours) vet is for every client.

  • Heat. Never leave a dog in a car. Walk early or late in hot weather, carry water, and test pavement (sidewalk) with the back of your hand for about 7 seconds. Heavy panting, drooling, weakness or collapse can mean heatstroke: cool the dog with cool, not ice cold, water and call a vet urgently.
  • Toxic foods. Chocolate, grapes and raisins, xylitol (in some sugar-free gum and peanut butter), onions, garlic and some medications are dangerous to dogs. Lilies are very dangerous to cats.
  • A swollen belly with retching that brings nothing up in a large dog can be bloat, which is an emergency.
  • If a pet goes missing, call the owner, search immediately and contact local shelters, the microchip database and animal control or the council.

If you are bitten, wash the wound, seek medical care for yourself and follow local reporting rules. First aid is only what you do while you call the vet.

How much to charge for dog walking

Rates vary enormously between towns, so work out your price from your own time and costs, then compare it with local rates.

  1. Time a real visit door to door. A 30-minute walk often takes 50 to 60 minutes once travel, keys, harnessing, drying and visit notes are counted.
  2. Choose a target hourly rate for your time.
  3. Estimate running costs per visit: fuel or fares, insurance, waste bags, treats, phone, gear and booking or card fees.
  4. Multiply the full time by your hourly rate and add the running costs.

Add a minimum charge so short visits still cover travel, and a travel fee or a polite no for homes outside your area. Price extras separately, such as extra pets in the same home, a second daily visit or bathing a very muddy dog. Holiday and last-minute surcharges are common and fair if they are written in your price list. Review prices yearly and give clients written notice of any rise.

Keys, meet-and-greets and contracts

Hold a meet-and-greet before every new client. Ask about the pet's temperament, health, routine, triggers and recall, then meet the pet in person and watch how it reacts to you. Saying no politely to an animal you cannot handle safely is part of being professional.

Collect a client information form, emergency contacts, vet details and the signed vet release. Then use a written service agreement covering services, schedule, prices, payment terms, cancellation policy, permissions such as off lead or group walks, photo permission and your key policy. Having a lawyer check your templates once is worth it.

Key and home security habits

  • Label keys with codes, never a name or address, and log every handover and return
  • Use lockboxes only with written permission and store alarm codes securely
  • Never post photos that reveal a client is away or show where they live
  • Do not bring anyone else into a client's home

How to find dog walking clients

Start with people who already know you. Tell friends, neighbors and coworkers what you offer and ask them to share your details. Many pet sitters find their first clients on their own street.

  • Set up your free local map listing and a simple website with services and service area
  • Post in local social media groups, following each group's rules
  • Pin flyers on noticeboards where allowed
  • Introduce yourself to vets, groomers and pet shops who get asked for recommendations
  • Consider a pet sitter marketplace while you build local reviews

Send clients short updates with photos after each visit, with permission, and ask happy clients for a review. Reliable updates, tidy homes and dogs returned calm and dry are what make owners recommend you. Plan cover for illness and keep some rest days, because a tired walker makes mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need insurance to be a dog walker?

Yes, you should have specialist pet care business insurance before your first client. It typically includes liability cover and cover for animals in your care. Read the exclusions carefully, since lost keys, vet bills and dog bites may not be included. In the US and Canada, many walkers also carry a bond, which protects clients against theft.

Can I start a dog walking business with no experience?

You can, but build experience first by caring for friends' pets, volunteering at a shelter and taking a pet first aid course. Start with solo walks for calm dogs, one household at a time, and learn dog body language thoroughly. Avoid group walks, puppies with incomplete vaccinations and reactive dogs until you have real confidence and a record of safe walks.

How long is a typical dog walk?

Many dog walkers offer 30-minute and 60-minute walks, but the right length depends on the dog's age, breed, health and the owner's wishes. Puppies and older dogs often need shorter, gentler outings. Remember that a 30-minute walk can take close to an hour of your working time once travel and handover are included, which affects your pricing.

What should be in a dog walking contract?

A dog walking service agreement should list your services, schedule, prices, payment terms and cancellation policy. Add emergency contacts, vet details and a vet release form, plus written permissions for off lead walking, group walks and photos. Include your key policy and liability limits where lawful. Asking a lawyer to check your template once is a sensible step.

What do I do if a dog gets lost on a walk?

Call the owner immediately, then start searching the area right away while staying safe near roads. Contact local shelters, the microchip database and animal control or the local council. Share a clear description and photo with the owner's agreement. Afterwards, review what happened, such as harness fit or a missing double clip, so it cannot happen again.

Is dog walking a good side hustle?

Dog walking can fit well around school hours, shifts or retirement, and startup costs are usually modest. It still needs proper insurance, local permits where required, records for taxes and real commitment, since dogs need walking in every weather and holidays are the busiest time. Starting small lets you test the work before scaling up.

Your first step today is to call or check the website of your city, county or council and ask whether commercial dog walkers need a permit or have a dog limit. While you wait for answers, ask a specialist insurer for a quote and draft your short services list. With those three things in hand, you are ready to book your first meet-and-greet.
