How to Start a Handyman Business: A Safe, Legal 8-Step Plan

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If friends already call you when a shelf falls down or a door sticks, you may be wondering whether people would pay you for the same help. To start a handyman business, choose a short list of small jobs you do well, check your local licensing rules, get liability insurance, build a price from your real costs, and then tell your neighbors you are open for work.

This guide walks you through each of those steps in order, with checklists and a worked pricing example. It covers the parts most new handymen (or handypersons, odd job people) skip, such as which jobs legally belong to licensed trades and how to write a quote that prevents arguments later.

Is a handyman business right for you

A handyman business is mostly small jobs for many different customers. One day you might assemble a wardrobe, hang three mirrors and fix a sticking cabinet door. The next you might patch a hole in drywall (plasterboard) and replace a rotten fence panel. Variety is the appeal, but it also means constant switching between tasks, tools and customers.

Be honest with yourself about the parts that are not repairs. You will work inside people's homes, often with them watching. You will spend real time on quotes, invoices, buying materials and driving. And the work is physical, with ladders, lifting and kneeling most days.

A quick skills audit

  • Which jobs do you already do well, and how long do they take you?
  • Which jobs do you avoid or still find tricky?
  • Are you comfortable talking to strangers and explaining costs?
  • Can you keep simple records and reply to messages promptly?

Many people start as a side business on evenings and weekends, then grow it once they have steady customers. That is a sensible way to test demand without giving up a paycheck.

Choose the handyman services you offer

A clear, short services list sells better than a promise to do anything. Customers want to know quickly whether you can help, and a focused list also keeps you away from work you are not qualified for.

Common handyman jobs that suit a new business include:

  • Assembling flat-pack furniture
  • Hanging shelves, pictures, mirrors and curtain rails
  • Patching drywall or plasterboard and small painting touch-ups
  • Replacing caulk or sealant around baths, sinks and showers
  • Adjusting doors, hinges and cabinet fronts
  • Fence and deck repairs
  • Weatherproofing and draft-proofing
  • Gutter cleaning from safe heights and small garden jobs

Write each service in plain words, the way a customer would describe the problem. Keep a few services in reserve that you only offer to existing customers until you have more practice. Review the list every few months and drop jobs that always run over time or that you dread.

Which jobs need a licensed tradesperson

This is the step that protects your customers, your reputation and you. Some work is legally reserved for licensed or registered trades in most places, and doing it without the right qualification can put people at risk and leave you uninsured.

Do not offer these unless you hold the proper license or registration:

  • Gas work of any kind. In the UK this legally requires Gas Safe registration; elsewhere a licensed gas fitter.
  • Most electrical work beyond simple like-for-like replacements where local rules allow. In England, notifiable work such as new circuits or work in bathrooms falls under building regulations.
  • Major plumbing such as new supply or drain lines, water heaters and boilers
  • Structural work, including removing walls
  • Roofing and other work at height
  • Anything needing a building permit or building regulations approval

When a customer asks for one of these, say so clearly and refer them to a licensed tradesperson you trust. Start a referral list now with an electrician, a plumber, a gas engineer and a roofer. Trades often return the favor by sending you the small jobs they do not want.

Do I need a license to be a handyman

It depends entirely on where you work, so check before your first paid job. In many US states, handyman work is allowed without a contractor license only for minor jobs below a set dollar value per job, and some states and cities require registration or a license for any paid work. Canadian provinces and Australian states and territories set their own rules, and in Australia and New Zealand much building, electrical and plumbing work must be done by licensed or registered people. The UK has no general handyman license, but specific work is regulated.

How to check your local rules

  1. Find out who regulates building work in your state, province, council or territory.
  2. Search their guidance for handyman, minor work or small jobs limits.
  3. Check each service on your list against those rules.
  4. Check whether your city or county needs a general business license.
  5. Write down what you found, with the date, and keep it with your business records.

If you are unsure, call the regulator and ask. This is general information, not legal advice.

Insurance, registration and taxes before day one

Get insurance before you touch a customer's home. A dropped drill through a window or a leak after you fit a tap part can cost far more than a year of premiums.

Handyman tools and safety essentials

Buy for the jobs on your list, not for every job you might one day do. A solid set of hand tools, a good cordless drill and driver, a level, a tape measure and a decent ladder will cover most early work. Rent or borrow anything you only need once a year.

Two testing tools belong in every kit: a non-contact voltage tester and a stud, pipe and cable detector. Turn power off at the breaker or fuse box and test before touching any fitting, and scan every wall before you drill.

Safety checklist before every job

  • Inspect the ladder, set it at about a 4 to 1 angle and keep three points of contact
  • Wear eye, ear, hand and dust protection suited to the task
  • Protect floors and furniture and keep children and pets away from the work
  • Carry a first aid kit and tell someone where you are working
  • Call the free dig hotline (811 in the US) before digging for posts
Asbestos may be present in buildings built or refurbished before about 2000 in the UK, Australia and New Zealand, and before about the 1980s in the US and Canada, in things like textured ceilings, old floor tiles and insulation. Do not drill, sand or remove suspect materials; stop and advise professional testing. In the US, paid work that disturbs paint in homes built before 1978 requires certification under federal lead-safe renovation rules.

How much to charge for handyman work

Most new handymen undercharge because they price by feel and forget the hours they are not paid for. Build your hourly rate from your own numbers, then compare it with local rates. Do not simply copy the cheapest handyman in town.

  1. Choose the yearly income you want to take home.
  2. Add yearly business costs: insurance, vehicle, fuel, tools, phone, advertising and accounting.
  3. Add money set aside for taxes, using your accountant's advice.
  4. Estimate billable hours honestly, allowing for travel, quoting, shopping, admin, holidays and quiet weeks.
  5. Divide the total by your billable hours.

Example only, with made-up round numbers: take-home income 30,000, plus costs 10,000, plus taxes 10,000, makes 50,000. At 40 working weeks of 25 billable hours, that is 1,000 hours, so the rate is 50 per hour.

Add a minimum charge or call-out fee so small visits still cover travel and setup. Once you know your real times for common tasks, you can offer flat rates, such as a set price to hang a curtain rail on a standard wall.

Write quotes that prevent arguments

Use photo quotes for small, familiar jobs and site visits for larger or uncertain ones. Break every job into clear tasks. Fix the bathroom is not a task; remove old sealant, clean and dry the surface, and apply new white silicone is.

A quote is a fixed price for a defined job, while an estimate is your best guess that may change. Say which one you are giving. A good written quote includes:

  • Your business details and any license or registration number
  • The task list in plain words, with materials shown separately
  • What is not included, such as disposal of old items
  • Timing, payment terms, any deposit, and how long the quote is valid

When the customer adds work on the day, agree the extra price in writing before you start it. Check local consumer rules on deposits and cooling-off periods for contracts signed in the customer's home, and consider asking a lawyer to look over your template. How to find your first handyman customers

Your first customers are usually people who already know and trust you. Tell friends, family, neighbors and coworkers what you now offer and ask them to pass your number on. Then widen the circle step by step.

  • Put your name and number on your truck or van; a marked vehicle builds trust at the door
  • Post on neighborhood apps and local social media groups, following each group's rules
  • Set up your free local map listing with your services list and service area
  • Pin flyers on community noticeboards where allowed
  • Contact landlords, property managers and real estate (estate) agents who need reliable small repairs

Customer service is what turns one job into ten. Arrive when you said you would, protect floors, clean up, explain what you did and give honest advice. Take before and after photos with permission, then ask happy customers for a short review. Be extra careful with older or vulnerable customers, and never pressure anyone into more work.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start a handyman business with no experience?

You need practical skills before charging money, but you do not need a trade qualification for many small jobs. Start with tasks you already do well at home, such as furniture assembly or hanging shelves, and record how long each takes. Add new services only after practicing them, and refer anything electrical, gas, structural or permit-related to licensed trades.

What insurance does a handyman need?

Liability insurance, called public liability in the UK and Australia, is the essential first policy because it covers damage or injury you cause on a job. Most handymen also add tools and equipment cover and vehicle insurance for business use. Once you employ someone, employer's liability or workers' compensation rules usually apply. Ask a broker what fits your services.

Should a handyman charge by the hour or by the job?

Most use both. Hourly pricing suits uncertain repairs where you cannot see the full problem in advance. Flat rates per task work well once you know your real times, and customers like knowing the price. Whichever you use, add a minimum charge so short visits still cover your travel, setup and admin time.

What jobs can a handyman not do?

Rules vary by location, but handymen generally cannot do gas work, most electrical work, major plumbing like water heaters and new drain lines, structural changes, roofing, or anything needing a building permit unless they hold the right license. Some US states also cap the dollar value of jobs an unlicensed handyman can take. Check your local regulator before quoting.

How do I get handyman clients fast?

Start with people who already know you, then add a free local map listing, posts in neighborhood groups and a marked vehicle. Landlords and property managers are worth contacting because they need small repairs often. Reply quickly, turn up on time and ask every happy customer for a review, since reviews and referrals bring most repeat work.

Is a handyman business a good side hustle?

It can be, because you can take jobs on evenings and weekends and start with tools you may already own. You still need insurance, any required registration and proper records from the first paid job. Starting part time lets you test demand, learn your real job times and build reviews before deciding whether to go full time.

Your first step today is simple: write your services list on one page, mark anything that belongs to a licensed trade, and look up who regulates building work where you live. With that page and one call to an insurance broker, you will know exactly what you can legally offer and be ready to price your first job.
