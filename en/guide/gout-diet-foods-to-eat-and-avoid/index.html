Gout Diet: Foods to Eat and Avoid, Plus What to Drink

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you have just had a gout flare, or your doctor has told you your uric acid is high, you probably want to know what you can still put on your plate. The short answer: limit organ meats, game, some oily fish and shellfish, meat extracts, beer, spirits and sugary drinks; keep red meat to small portions; and enjoy vegetables, beans, lentils, eggs, low-fat dairy, whole grains and plenty of water.

Food helps, but it is only part of the story, because most uric acid is made by your own body. Below you will find a simple food guide, the drinks that matter most, a plate you can build without weighing, a sample day, what to do during a flare and the warning signs that need urgent care.

What causes gout and how food fits in

Gout is a type of arthritis caused by too much uric acid in the blood. When there is more than your kidneys can clear, it can form tiny, needle-shaped crystals in and around a joint. When something sets them off, you get a flare: sudden, severe pain, swelling, redness and heat, often at night and classically in the big toe, though ankles, knees, wrists, fingers and elbows can be affected too.

Your body makes uric acid when it breaks down purines, natural substances found in your own cells and in many foods and drinks. Here is the surprising part: most uric acid is made by your body, not taken in from food. So diet matters, but it is not the whole story.

Other risk factors include your genes, kidney function, high blood pressure, some medicines such as some water pills (diuretics), carrying extra weight, alcohol and sugary drinks. If you take a medicine linked to gout, do not stop it. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist.

Gout diet food list to stick on the fridge

Think of food in three groups. Enjoy often means eat freely as part of normal meals. Moderation means sensible portions and not every day. Limit means keep it rare, or skip it.

Best and worst drinks for gout

If you change only one thing, change what is in your glass.

  • Water: about 8 cups (2 liters) a day as a general guide, more in hot weather or when active. It helps your kidneys clear uric acid. If your doctor has told you to limit fluids because of heart or kidney problems, follow their advice instead.
  • Beer: the drink most strongly linked to higher uric acid, and alcohol-free beer may still contain purines. Spirits are linked to flares too.
  • Wine seems less strongly linked but still counts. Stay within your country's low-risk guidance at most, and avoid alcohol completely during a flare.
  • Sugary sodas (fizzy drinks), sweetened iced teas and energy drinks, especially those made with high fructose corn syrup (glucose-fructose syrup in the UK), are linked to higher uric acid. Swap them for water, sparkling water or unsweetened tea.
  • Fruit juice is concentrated fruit sugar. Keep it to a small glass now and then and eat whole fruit instead.
  • Coffee: studies suggest moderate coffee is not harmful for most people with gout. There is no need to start drinking it for gout.

A simple trick: fill a 1 liter bottle twice a day. If the first one is empty by lunchtime, you are on track.

How to eat less meat without going hungry

You do not have to give up meat. Make it a supporting player instead of the star, and give some days over entirely to plant proteins. Beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, eggs and low-fat dairy all bring filling protein with fewer purines. Studies suggest low-fat milk and yogurt may be linked to lower uric acid, so a yogurt at lunch is a pleasant habit to build.

Use this plate as a guide, no weighing needed:

  • Half: vegetables or salad.
  • A quarter: protein, such as beans, eggs, tofu or a palm-sized piece of chicken or fish.
  • A quarter: starch, such as potatoes, rice, pasta or whole grain bread.
  • A glass of water alongside.

Stretch smaller amounts of meat by bulking dishes out. A chicken stir-fry packed with vegetables goes further than a chicken breast on its own, and a chili made with three kinds of beans needs no meat at all.

Reading labels for hidden purines and sugar

A few ingredients hide in everyday products:

  • Sweeteners such as high fructose corn syrup, glucose-fructose syrup, fructose and fruit juice concentrate, in drinks, sauces and cereals.
  • Meat extracts in stock cubes, bouillon powders, gravy granules and some soups and ready meals.
  • Yeast extract in savory spreads, some crackers and stock powders.
  • Anchovies in some steak sauces, brown table sauces, salad dressings and pastes.

For broth (stock), choose a vegetable version without meat or yeast extract, or make your own by simmering onion, carrot, celery, garlic and herbs in water for about an hour. Also watch for swapping beer for big glasses of juice or smoothies, which are loaded with fruit sugar.

A sample day of gout-friendly meals

Here is what an ordinary day might look like, with no special products:

MealIdea
BreakfastSavory oatmeal (porridge) with a little cheddar and scallions (spring onions), plus a glass of low-fat milk
LunchLentil and vegetable soup with whole grain bread and a pot of low-fat yogurt
SnackA piece of fruit and a small handful of nuts
DinnerLemon herb chicken (a palm-sized piece) roasted on a tray with potatoes and plenty of vegetables
DrinksWater through the day, tea or coffee if you already enjoy them

Cook chicken to 165°F (74°C) and ground (minced) meat to 160°F (71°C). Batch-cook soups and freeze them in portions, so you have something easy ready for a bad day. The Gout Diet Cookbook Made Simple includes a four-week meal plan in which red meat appears no more than once a week.

What to eat during a gout flare

A flare can arrive overnight, so have a plan ready before you need it.

  • Rest the joint and raise it on a pillow.
  • Hold a cold pack or a bag of frozen peas wrapped in a towel against it for up to about 20 minutes at a time. Never put ice straight on the skin.
  • Keep bedclothes off a painful foot with a frame, a box or a pillow under the covers.
  • Take the flare medicine your doctor recommended, as early as possible and exactly as advised.
  • Avoid alcohol completely and keep sipping water.

Keep meals simple, light and low in purines, ideally from the freezer: tomato and white bean soup, a baked potato with cottage cheese and chives, an egg salad sandwich or pasta with a simple tomato sauce. A short flare diary (date, joint, what you ate and drank, medicine) helps you and your doctor spot patterns.

Get urgent same-day medical care for a hot, swollen, painful joint with fever or chills, or if you feel generally very unwell, because it can be a joint infection. Seek urgent help too for a first-ever flare that has not been diagnosed, severe pain that does not settle, or signs of a kidney stone (severe pain in your side or back, blood in your urine). Call 911 (US, Canada), 999 (UK), 000 (Australia) or 111 (New Zealand) in an emergency.

Why diet alone is often not enough

Eating fewer high purine foods, cutting back on alcohol and sugary drinks, drinking plenty of water and eating more plants and low-fat dairy may lower uric acid a little and may make flares less likely for some people. These habits are good for your heart and blood pressure too. But no food, juice, vinegar or powder will dissolve crystals or flush out gout.

Many people need long-term urate-lowering medicine, usually taken every day, to keep uric acid low and prevent flares and joint damage. When you first start it, you may get more flares for a while as crystals break up, which is why doctors often prescribe a second medicine for the early months. Keep taking it during a flare unless your doctor says otherwise.

If your doctor advises losing weight, do it gradually. Crash diets, fasting and very low carbohydrate, high protein diets can raise uric acid and trigger flares.

Talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before big diet changes, especially if you have kidney disease, diabetes or heart disease. Never stop or change any medicine, including blood pressure tablets or water pills, without speaking to your doctor or pharmacist.

Frequently asked questions

What foods trigger gout flares?

The foods and drinks most linked with flares are organ meats, game, some seafood such as anchovies, sardines, herring, mackerel and mussels, meat extracts and rich gravies, beer, spirits and sugary drinks made with fructose. Large portions of red meat matter too. Flares can also follow illness, injury, dehydration or a change in medicine, so a flare diary helps you see your own pattern.

Are eggs good for gout?

Yes, eggs are a low purine protein and a handy swap for meat. Scrambled eggs, an omelet with spinach and mushrooms, a vegetable frittata or a breakfast burrito with eggs and black beans all fit a gout-friendly diet. Cook egg dishes to 160°F (71°C). If you have other conditions such as high cholesterol, ask your doctor how often they suit you.

Can I eat chicken with gout?

Most people with gout can eat chicken and turkey in sensible, palm-sized portions, about 3 to 4 oz (85 to 115 g) cooked. Avoid chicken livers and rich meat gravies, and bulk dishes out with vegetables, beans or grains. Try to have some meat-free days each week too, using eggs, beans, lentils, tofu and low-fat dairy for protein.

Does cherry juice or apple cider vinegar help gout?

No single juice, vinegar or food has been shown to cure gout, stop flares or dissolve crystals. Juices also add fruit sugar, which can work against you. The habits with the best support are drinking plenty of water, cutting back on alcohol and sugary drinks, keeping high purine foods occasional and taking any medicine your doctor prescribes.

How much water should I drink with gout?

About 8 cups (2 liters) a day is a sensible general guide, more in hot weather or when you are active, because water helps your kidneys clear uric acid. Spread it through the day rather than drinking it all at once. If your doctor has told you to limit fluids, for example because of heart or kidney problems, follow their advice instead.

Can diet alone cure gout?

No. Food changes may lower uric acid a little and make flares less likely for some people, but most uric acid is made by your own body. Many people need daily urate-lowering medicine prescribed by a doctor to keep levels low and protect their joints. Diet works best alongside treatment, not instead of it, and it supports your heart and blood pressure too.

Start today with your glass: fill a 1 liter bottle with water, aim to empty it twice, and swap any beer or sugary drinks for sparkling water or unsweetened tea. Then pin the food guide above to your fridge and plan two meat-free dinners for this week.
