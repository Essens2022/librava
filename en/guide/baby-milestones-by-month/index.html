Baby Milestones by Month: What to Expect in the First Year

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you have ever found yourself comparing your baby with a friend's baby and quietly worrying, you are in very good company. The short answer is this: most babies smile socially around 6 to 8 weeks, roll around 4 to 6 months, sit alone between 6 and 9 months, crawl between 7 and 10 months and walk anywhere from 9 to 18 months, and every one of those is a range, not a deadline.

This guide walks you through what many babies do month by month in the first year, how to support each stage with simple everyday play, and which signs mean it is worth a chat with your doctor, pediatrician or health visitor.

How baby milestones really work

Milestones describe what many babies can do by a certain age. They are not a test your baby passes or fails. Two healthy babies born on the same day can smile, roll and babble weeks apart, and both can be perfectly on track.

What matters most is steady progress over time. A baby who is a little late on one skill and early on another is showing completely normal development. Your provider looks at the whole picture across several checkups, not a single day.

Premature babies and adjusted age

If your baby was born early, count their age from their due date rather than their birth date. A baby born six weeks early who is now three months old may be doing what most six-week-olds do, and that is right where they should be. Providers usually use adjusted age until around age two.

Newborn to 3 months milestones

In the first month, life is mostly feeding, sleeping and being held. Your newborn sees best at about 8 to 12 inches (20 to 30 cm), roughly the distance to your face during a feed. They already know your voice and smell, and they prefer faces and bold black and white patterns over soft pastels.

You will also see reflexes they were born with. The startle reflex makes them fling out their arms at a sudden noise, and the rooting reflex makes them turn toward a touch on the cheek looking for a feed. These fade over the coming months as more deliberate movement takes over.

What many babies do by about 3 months

  • First social smile, usually between 6 and 8 weeks, in response to your face or voice
  • Cooing with soft vowel sounds like ooh and aah, and cooing back when you talk
  • Lifting their head briefly on their tummy, then pushing up on their forearms
  • Bringing their hands to their mouth and opening and closing their fists
  • Following a face or moving toy with their eyes and turning toward sounds

Crying often peaks around 6 to 8 weeks and eases by about 3 to 4 months.

Baby milestones at 4 to 6 months

This is when many parents say their baby suddenly switched on. Babies start smiling to get your attention, laugh out loud, squeal and blow raspberries. By around 6 months, many babble strings like ba-ba and love simple games like peekaboo.

Movement speeds up too. Many babies roll from tummy to back between 4 and 6 months, and from back to tummy a little later, around 5 to 7 months. Some skip rolling almost completely, which is fine. By 6 months, many push up on straight arms and lean forward on their hands to sit for a few moments.

Hands become busy tools. Your baby reaches, grasps with the whole hand, passes toys from one hand to the other and puts everything in their mouth. Mouthing is how they explore, so small objects need to move up high now.

Rolling changes the safety rules. Once your baby can roll, never leave them alone on a bed, sofa or changing table, even for a second, and stop swaddling for sleep. Keep placing them on their back for every sleep in a clear crib (cot), and keep sharing a room for at least the first 6 months.

Sleep often wobbles around 4 months as sleep cycles mature. Most babies still sleep around 12 to 15 hours in 24 hours, including roughly three naps, and night feeds are still normal.

Baby milestones at 7 to 9 months

Many babies sit steadily without support somewhere between 6 and 9 months. Having both hands free for play changes everything, and you will notice your baby exploring objects with real concentration.

Crawling often starts between 7 and 10 months, but it comes in many styles. Some babies crawl on hands and knees, some bottom shuffle, some commando crawl on their tummies and some roll across the room. A few skip crawling and go straight to pulling up. All of these can be normal ways to get around.

Thinking and talking

Around this age, babies learn that things still exist when hidden, so they start lifting a cloth to find a toy you covered. Babbling becomes richer, with mamama and bababa, and many babies turn when they hear their name by about 9 months. They also start using gestures like lifting their arms to be picked up.

Between about 8 and 10 months, separation anxiety often arrives. Your baby may cry the moment you leave the room. It feels intense, but it shows a strong bond. Keep goodbyes short and cheerful, and always come back when you say you will.

Baby milestones at 10 to 12 months

Many babies pull up to stand on furniture between 8 and 12 months, then start cruising, which means walking sideways while holding the sofa or a low table. Independent steps often come between 9 and 18 months. A baby who walks at 15 months is just as typical as one who walks at 10 months.

Fine motor skills sharpen as well. By 9 to 12 months, many babies use a pincer grasp to pick up small things between thumb and first finger. They drop and throw things on purpose, fill and empty containers and may point at what interests them.

First words

Your baby understands far more than they can say. By 12 months, many follow simple instructions like give me the ball, especially with a gesture. First words usually arrive between 10 and 14 months, often mama, dada or the name of a pet or favorite food. Some babies have a few words by their first birthday and others have none yet but babble in long, sentence-like strings. Both are within the normal range.

Indoors, bare feet are best for learning to walk. Skip wheeled baby walkers, which many health organizations advise against because they let babies reach hazards and stairs quickly and do not help them walk sooner.

How to help your baby reach milestones

You do not need flashcards, special programs or expensive toys. At this age, you are the best toy in the house, and everyday moments do the work.

Tummy time from the first days

When your baby is awake and supervised, short spells on their tummy build neck, shoulder and back strength and help prevent a flat spot on the head. Start with a few minutes, two or three times a day, and build up slowly. By around 3 months, many babies manage 30 minutes or more spread across the day.

  • Lie your baby on your chest while you recline, so they can look at your face.
  • Get down on the floor at their eye level and talk or sing.
  • Try it after a diaper (nappy) change rather than straight after a feed.
  • Stop when they have had enough and try again later.

Talk, pause and answer

Narrate your day: now we are putting on your socks, one sock, two socks. When your baby coos or babbles, pause and wait, then answer as if you are chatting. That back and forth is the start of conversation. Later, name what they point at and add a word to their sounds, so ba becomes ball, a red ball.

Simple play ideas by age

  • 0 to 3 months: faces, songs, high-contrast pictures, gentle leg cycling at changes
  • 4 to 6 months: peekaboo, textured toys, a baby-safe mirror, copying their sounds
  • 7 to 9 months: hiding a toy under a cloth, a basket of safe household items
  • 10 to 12 months: board books every day, push-along toys with a stable base, songs with actions

Watch for signs your baby needs a break, such as looking away, arching their back, yawning or fussing. Young babies get overstimulated quickly, and a quiet cuddle counts as development too. Many health organizations also advise avoiding screen time for babies, apart from video calls with family.

When to worry about baby milestones

Most worries turn out to be normal variation, but you know your baby best, and asking early is always fine. Talk to your doctor, pediatrician or health visitor if your baby:

  • By about 3 months, does not react to loud sounds, watch faces, smile at people or hold their head up at all
  • By about 6 months, does not laugh or squeal, reach for toys, roll in either direction or turn toward voices
  • By about 9 months, does not sit with help, babble, respond to their name or bear any weight on their legs when held standing
  • By about 12 months, does not move around in any way, stand with support, wave or point, or say any sounds like mama or dada
  • At any age, seems very stiff or very floppy, uses only one side of their body, or loses a skill they used to have

Health problems come before milestones. This guide is general information, not medical advice. Call your doctor or emergency number (911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK) straight away if your baby under 3 months has a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or above, struggles to breathe, has blue or very pale lips, is unusually drowsy or floppy, is not feeding, or has a rash that does not fade under a glass.

Frequently asked questions

When do babies start smiling?

Most babies give their first real social smile between 6 and 8 weeks, sometimes a little earlier or later. It is a smile in response to your face or voice, not the fleeting twitch you see during sleep. Once it starts, it shows up more and more. If your baby is not smiling at people by around 3 months, mention it to your provider at the next checkup.

When do babies roll over?

Many babies roll from tummy to back between 4 and 6 months, and from back to tummy a little later, around 5 to 7 months. Some babies skip rolling almost completely and move on to sitting or crawling. Once your baby rolls, stop swaddling for sleep and never leave them alone on a raised surface such as a bed or changing table.

When do babies sit up on their own?

Most babies sit steadily without support somewhere between 6 and 9 months. Before that, around 6 months, many lean forward on their hands to sit for a few moments. You can help by giving plenty of floor play and tummy time. If your baby cannot sit even with help by about 9 months, talk to your provider.

Is it normal for a baby not to crawl?

Yes. Some babies never crawl in the classic hands and knees way. They bottom shuffle, commando crawl, roll across the room or go straight to pulling up and cruising. What matters is that your baby finds some way to get around by about 12 months. If they are not moving around in any way by then, check in with your provider.

When should a baby say their first word?

First words usually come between 10 and 14 months, often mama, dada or a word for something they love. Many babies say none by their first birthday but babble in long, chatty strings, which is also normal. Read together daily, name things they look at and give them time to respond. Talk to your provider if there are no sounds like mama or dada by around 12 months.

Should I use adjusted age for a premature baby?

Yes. For babies born early, providers usually measure milestones from the due date rather than the birth date, and they often keep doing this until around age two. So a baby born six weeks early is compared with babies six weeks younger. This gives a fair picture and saves you a lot of unnecessary worry.

Today, pick three moments that already happen in your day, such as after a diaper change, after a bath and before an evening feed, and turn each into a few minutes of floor play and chat at your baby's eye level. Watch what your baby can do this week rather than what the chart says, jot down any questions, and take them to your next checkup.
