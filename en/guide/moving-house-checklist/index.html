Moving House Checklist: An 8-Week Plan From First Box to First Night

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

You have a moving date, a home full of stuff and a growing sense that you should have started already. The simplest moving house checklist spreads the work over eight weeks: decide and book in weeks 8 and 7, start admin and declutter in weeks 6 and 5, notify everyone and pack steadily in weeks 4 to 2, then finish, clean and carry a first-night box in moving week.

Below you will find that countdown week by week, plus how to choose movers safely, packing rules that prevent broken dishes, the address change list and a calm plan for moving day itself. Less than eight weeks? There is a short-notice version too.

Your 8-week moving checklist at a glance

Eight weeks sounds like ages, and it is, as long as you use it. The trick is two or three focused one-hour sessions a week rather than a frantic final weekend.

Weeks leftMain focusKey tasks
8Decide and gatherChoose movers or DIY, request quotes, read your lease, get boxes
7Declutter and bookBook movers or a van in writing, clear storage areas, list items to sell
6Start the adminSchools, medical records, utilities research, insurance check
5Pack the rarely usedOut-of-season clothes, books, decorations, donation drop-off
4Notify and confirmMail forwarding, bank and employer, utility dates, parking permit
3Pack in earnestMost of the kitchen, dining room and office, take apart spare furniture
2Loose endsReconfirm bookings, use up freezer food, book the end-of-tenancy clean
Moving weekFinishDefrost fridge, pack a suitcase, final walk-through

Find your current week, read back one or two weeks to catch anything missed and start there. Cross out what does not apply to you and add anything specific, such as a piano mover or a pet sitter.

Weeks 8 to 5, plan, book and declutter

Early weeks are about decisions. Set up one moving hub, a folder or notebook where every quote, contract and list lives, then work through these tasks.

  • Pick your approach. Full-service movers, a mixed approach (you pack, hired help loads or drives) or full DIY with friends and a rented van.
  • Request at least three written quotes, or price van and truck rental. Book early, because summer weekends and month-end dates fill up fast.
  • If you rent, read your lease for notice periods and end-of-tenancy rules, and give notice in writing if needed.
  • Declutter before you pack, starting with the attic (loft), basement, garage or spare room. Post larger items for sale now so buyers have time to collect.
  • Measure doorways and main rooms in the new home if you can, so you do not move furniture that will not fit.
  • Week 6 admin: contact the new school if you have children, ask how medical and vet records transfer, research utilities, and check whether your home, renters or contents insurance covers belongings in transit.
  • Week 5: start packing things you will not miss for a month.

Also create a do-not-pack zone: one shelf or closet for passports, documents, chargers, medications and keys. Tell everyone in the house that nothing there goes in a box.

How to choose movers and avoid scams

The cheapest quote can easily become the most expensive move. For anything bigger than a studio, ask for an in-home or video survey rather than a phone guess, and make sure each written quote covers the same things:

  • Date and arrival window
  • What is included: packing, materials, furniture disassembly, stairs, long carries
  • Whether the price is fixed or an estimate, and what could change it
  • Insurance or protection included, and the cost of fuller cover
  • Deposit, payment schedule and cancellation policy

Read reviews on several independent sites and look for patterns, not single complaints. Ask whether their own staff do the move or subcontract it, and check registration where your country requires it.

Be cautious of any mover that wants a large deposit in cash or by an untraceable method, quotes far below everyone else without seeing your things, has no physical address or written contract, uses different names on the phone and paperwork, or pressures you to sign fast. Pay by card or traceable payment where you can. If something feels wrong, walk away while your belongings are still in your home. This is general guidance; check your contract and local consumer rules.

Basic protection in a moving quote is often limited and sometimes calculated by weight, not value. Photograph furniture and valuables room by room before the move so any claim is easier.

Packing tips that save time and breakages

Gather supplies before you start: boxes in several sizes, strong packing tape (parcel tape in the UK), packing paper, bubble wrap, resealable bags for screws, several thick markers and colored labels. As a rough guide, a one-bedroom home needs 20 to 40 boxes, a two-bedroom 40 to 60 and a three-bedroom 60 to 90.

The golden rules:

  1. Heavy things in small boxes, light things in big boxes. Books go in small boxes; pillows and lampshades in large ones.
  2. Fill every box completely so nothing shifts when you gently shake it. Towels and clothes make free padding.
  3. Tape the bottom and top in an H shape: along the center seam, then across both edges.
  4. Keep each box to about 40 to 50 lb (18 to 23 kg) at most, less for anyone with back trouble.
  5. Pack one room at a time and never mix rooms in a box.

Label every box on two sides and the top with its room, specific contents and a number, and keep a numbered inventory. Give each room a color and tape a matching sign on each door in the new home, so helpers simply match colors. Snapping a photo of each box's contents before sealing it makes finding the cheese grater three weeks later easy.

Do not put fuels, gas cylinders, paint thinners, aerosols or fireworks on a moving truck. Carry documents, jewelry, medications, laptops and keys yourself.

Weeks 4 to 1, address changes and final packing

With a month to go, tell the world. Set up mail forwarding through your national postal service to start a day or two before moving day, because it often needs lead time. Then update your address in a sensible order:

  • Money and work: bank, cards, employer, pension or benefits office
  • Government: tax office, vehicle and driving license records, voter registration (electoral roll in the UK)
  • Home: utilities at both ends, home or contents insurance, internet
  • Health and family: doctor, dentist, schools, vet
  • Everyday life: subscriptions, deliveries, memberships

Book disconnection at the old home and connection at the new one, and photograph meter readings with the date visible on moving day. If street parking is tight, ask your city or local council about a permit for the truck a few weeks ahead.

In week 3, pack most of the kitchen. It has more small, breakable, awkward items than any other room and takes about twice as long as people expect. Keep out one pan, one pot and a plate, bowl, mug and cutlery set per person. In week 2, reconfirm every booking, eat down the freezer and arrange an end-of-tenancy clean and final inspection if you rent.

What to put in your first-night box

This is the single most useful box you will pack. Pack it last, label it on every side and travel with it in your own car. A clear plastic tub is ideal, because you can spot it among the cardboard.

  • Kettle or coffee maker, mugs, tea, coffee and long-life milk
  • Snacks, a few easy meals and a plate, bowl and cutlery per person
  • Toilet paper, hand soap, a towel per person and basic toiletries
  • Bedding and pillows for each bed
  • Chargers, a power strip (extension lead) and a flashlight (torch)
  • Screwdriver, box cutter, scissors and tape
  • Light bulbs, paper towels (kitchen roll) and cleaning wipes
  • Trash bags (bin bags)
  • Medicines and the essentials for children and pets

Alongside it, pack a suitcase as if for a short trip, with two or three days of clothes, toiletries and medication.

Moving day checklist, step by step

The night before: finish packing everything except overnight essentials, charge phones and power banks, defrost and dry the fridge, bag bed screws and tape each bag to its furniture, and confirm the arrival time. Then eat a proper dinner and go to bed.

On the day:

  1. Eat breakfast. The next proper meal may be hours away.
  2. Set up a clearly signed do-not-load zone for the first-night box, documents, valuables and children's comfort bags.
  3. Walk the crew or helpers through the house. Point out fragile items, heavy pieces and anything that is not coming. Check movers' paperwork matches what you agreed.
  4. Give one friend the coordinator role, and keep water and snacks by the door.
  5. When the truck is loaded, open every cupboard, closet, drawer and the oven, and check the loft, shed and behind doors.
  6. Photograph each empty room and the meters, lock up and hand over keys as agreed.
  7. At the new home, arrive first if you can, check heating, water and power, and stand at the door directing each box by its label.

Lift safely all day: bend your knees, keep loads close, avoid twisting, team lift big items and use a hand truck (sack truck) or dolly for heavy boxes. Anyone with back problems or a recent injury can take lighter jobs like labeling or minding children.

On the first night, make the beds, set up the bathroom and find the fuse box and water shutoff (stopcock). Unpacking can wait until tomorrow.

Moving at short notice

A new job or a lease that falls through can leave you with four weeks, or even two. The countdown still works; you simply squeeze it. With four weeks, combine weeks 8 and 7 into your first week, weeks 6 and 5 into your second, and so on.

With two weeks, focus on the essentials: book moving help on day one, set up mail forwarding and utilities straight away, and pack daily in short bursts. Declutter as you pack rather than as a separate step.

Ask for help early and be specific. A friend who gives you one Saturday to pack books can save you an entire evening, and a clear request (a date, a start time, a job) is much easier to say yes to. Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should I start preparing to move?

About eight weeks is comfortable for most households. That gives you time to compare movers, declutter, start admin like school places and utilities, and pack a little each week. Book movers or a rental van as early as possible, especially for summer weekends or month-end dates, which fill up quickly. Short notice is manageable if you combine weeks.

What should I pack first when moving?

Start with things you will not need for a month: out-of-season clothes, books you have read, holiday decorations, spare bedding, good china and decorative items. Storage areas like the attic, basement and garage come next. Pack daily essentials last, and keep important documents, medications, chargers and keys in a do-not-pack zone that travels with you.

How many boxes do I need to move?

As a rough guide for a decluttered home: 20 to 40 boxes for a studio or one-bedroom, 40 to 60 for two bedrooms, 60 to 90 for three bedrooms and 90 or more for larger homes. Add extra if you own lots of books or kitchen equipment. Having a few spare boxes is far better than running out late at night.

Who do I need to notify when I move house?

Set up mail forwarding first, then update your bank and cards, employer, pension or benefits office, tax office, vehicle and driving license records, and voter registration. Tell utility suppliers at both homes, your insurer and internet provider, plus your doctor, dentist, schools and vet. Finish with subscriptions, deliveries and memberships so nothing is sent to the old address.

How can I tell if a moving company is a scam?

Warning signs include a request for a large cash deposit, a quote far below every other company without seeing your belongings, no physical address or registration, different company names on the phone and paperwork, no written contract and pressure to sign quickly. Get at least three written quotes, read independent reviews and pay by a traceable method where possible.

What should I do on the first night in a new home?

Keep it simple. Make the beds, set up the bathroom with toilet paper, soap and towels, and use your first-night box for a hot drink and an easy meal. Check that doors and windows lock, and find the fuse box and water shutoff. If you have children, set up their rooms first so they sleep somewhere familiar. Unpacking can wait.

Your first step today: work out which week of the countdown you are in, set up a single moving folder, and request written quotes from three movers or rental firms. With those booked, every other task on the list has a date to work toward.
