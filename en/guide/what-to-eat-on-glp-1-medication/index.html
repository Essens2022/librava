What to Eat on GLP-1 Medication: Small Meals, Protein First

By the Librava editors · Updated 2026-10-26

If you have just started GLP-1 medication, you may find that a plate you used to finish now looks like a mountain, and some days nothing appeals at all. The short answer to what to eat: small, regular meals with protein first (eggs, chicken, fish, yogurt, beans or tofu), some soft vegetables and a little starch, plenty of fluids sipped through the day, and easy on greasy, fried and very sugary foods.

This guide covers how the medication changes hunger, the small plate method, what a protein portion looks like, fiber and fluids, food for queasy days, eating out and the symptoms that need urgent help. Your prescriber manages your medication, and nothing here replaces their advice.

How GLP-1 medication changes your appetite

In general terms, the medication can reduce appetite and slow how quickly your stomach empties. Food sits there longer, so you feel full sooner and stay full for longer. Many people also notice that constant thoughts about food (often called food noise) get quieter.

That slower stomach also explains the common side effects: nausea, feeling overly full, reflux (heartburn), burping, constipation, diarrhea and sometimes vomiting. They are often more noticeable when you start or after a dose change, and for many people they ease with time.

The challenge shifts from eating less to eating well on less. When portions shrink, every bite needs to count, and skipping meals altogether can leave you short on protein, fluids and nutrients.

Your medication is prescribed and managed by your doctor or prescriber. Never start, stop, skip or change a dose because of anything you read about food. Talk to your prescriber or pharmacist about side effects, and to a registered dietitian if you are eating very little for weeks, are pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney disease or have a history of eating disorders.

Use the small plate method

A full dinner plate piled high is discouraging when you can barely face food. Use a side plate about 7 to 8 inches (18 to 20 cm) across instead, and build it in this order:

  1. Protein first. Make it the biggest item on the plate.
  2. Vegetables next: a small handful, soft and well cooked on sensitive days.
  3. A little starch: a couple of spoonfuls of rice, potato, pasta or oats, if you have room.
  4. Flavor: lemon, herbs, a spoon of yogurt sauce or a pinch of spice.

Eat in that order too. If you fill up after the protein and a few vegetables, that is a good meal. Pick one item from each column below to build a plate without a recipe.

How much protein on GLP-1 in real portions

Protein at every meal and snack helps you keep muscle while you are eating less. You do not need scales. Use your hand and your eyes:

FoodSmall portion looks like
Chicken, turkey or lean meatA piece the size of your palm
Fish or shrimp (prawns)A palm-sized fillet or a small handful
Eggs2 eggs
Greek yogurtA small pot, about 2/3 cup (150 g)
Cottage cheeseAbout 1/2 cup (115 g)
TofuA palm-sized slab
Beans, chickpeas or lentils3 to 4 heaping spoonfuls
Hummus2 to 3 heaping spoonfuls

On days you can only manage half a portion, that is still worth having. Gentle strength activity, such as standing up from a chair several times or using light weights, also helps protect muscle. Check with your doctor first if you have a health condition. If you have kidney disease, ask your doctor or dietitian before increasing protein.

Protein-first breakfast and snack ideas

Breakfast is an easy place to start, because a protein-rich morning meal sets up the rest of the day. A few ideas that suit a small appetite:

  • Soft scrambled eggs stirred with a spoon of cottage cheese and chopped chives.
  • Savory oatmeal (porridge) topped with a soft egg and wilted greens.
  • Greek yogurt with cucumber, tomato and herbs, or with berries and seeds.
  • Smoked salmon and a boiled egg with a slice of whole grain toast.

For snacks, think of them as mini meals with protein in them: a hard-boiled egg with hummus, cottage cheese with cucumber, roasted spiced chickpeas, turkey and cheese roll-ups or apple slices with peanut butter. Five small chances to eat in a day often work better than three big ones you cannot finish.

Fiber and fluids for comfortable digestion

Constipation is common because everything moves more slowly. Add fiber gradually from vegetables, beans, lentils, oats and whole grains, and drink more as you do. Keep gently active, and speak to your pharmacist or doctor if constipation lasts more than a few days or is painful, rather than starting laxatives or supplements on your own.

When you eat less, you also get less fluid from food. General guidance for most adults is around 6 to 8 cups or glasses of fluid a day, about 1.5 to 2 liters, more in hot weather or when active, unless your doctor has told you to limit fluids. Water, milk, tea, coffee, broth and soup all count.

  • Keep a bottle or glass in sight and sip steadily through the day.
  • If drinking with meals makes you too full, stop about 30 minutes before eating and start again 30 minutes after.
  • Choose still drinks if fizzy ones cause bloating or burping.
  • Water-rich foods such as cucumber, melon, yogurt and soups help too.

Foods to go easy on with GLP-1

There are no good and bad foods here, only foods that many people find harder to handle with a slower stomach. Go easy on:

  • Greasy, fried and very rich or creamy dishes, which sit in the stomach longest.
  • Very spicy foods, especially if you get reflux.
  • Very sugary foods and drinks.
  • Fizzy drinks, if they bloat you.
  • Large portions and large drinks with meals.
  • Alcohol, which may worsen nausea or reflux and adds to dehydration. For people on diabetes medicines it can also raise the risk of low blood sugar. Follow your country's low-risk guidance and ask your prescriber.

None of these is banned forever. Notice which ones bother you personally and adjust.

What to eat when nauseous on GLP-1

Nausea is the side effect people mention most. Small habits help many people: eat small amounts often instead of going long stretches with an empty stomach, stop at the first sign of fullness, sip fluids slowly between meals and stay upright for a while after eating.

Cool or room-temperature foods give off less smell and are often easier to face: chilled cottage cheese, a hard-boiled egg or cold poached chicken with crackers. A gentle example meal is a ginger chicken and rice soup, simply poached chicken simmered in broth with rice, sliced ginger and soft carrots.

For reflux, finish your evening meal a few hours before bed and stay upright for 2 to 3 hours after eating. On diarrhea days, focus on replacing fluid with small, steady sips, and choose white rice, toast, bananas and plain chicken until things settle.

Eating out and planning for rough days

At a restaurant, order a starter (appetizer) as your main, share a dish or ask for sauces on the side. Eat the protein first and ask for a box for leftovers without apology. When someone urges you to have more, saying you are full and that it was lovely usually does the job.

At home, keep a gentle shelf stocked with plain crackers, rice, broth, ginger tea and a couple of frozen single portions of soup or stew. Freeze leftovers in small portions, eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days and reheat until steaming hot, only once. The Easy GLP-1 Cookbook has a full chapter of freezer-friendly single portions and four weeks of small meal plans.

If you also take insulin or certain other diabetes medicines, eating much less can raise the risk of low blood sugar. Know the signs (shaking, sweating, hunger, confusion, fast heartbeat) and follow your diabetes team's plan rather than skipping meals.

Get urgent medical help for severe stomach pain that will not go away (especially if it spreads to your back), vomiting that will not stop or being unable to keep fluids down, very dark or very little urine with dizziness or confusion, pain in the upper right belly with fever or yellowing skin or eyes, or swelling of the face, lips or throat. Call 911 (US, Canada), 999 (UK), 000 (Australia) or 111 (New Zealand).

Frequently asked questions

What foods should you avoid on GLP-1 medication?

No food is strictly forbidden, but many people find greasy, fried, very rich, very spicy and very sugary foods harder to handle, along with fizzy drinks and large portions. Alcohol may also worsen nausea and reflux. Notice what bothers you personally and cut back on those. Your prescriber can advise on anything specific to your medicines.

Is it okay to skip meals if I am not hungry?

Small regular meals usually work better than skipping. Going long stretches with an empty stomach can make nausea worse, and you may miss out on protein, fluids and nutrients. Aim for a few small chances to eat each day, even if one is just yogurt or an egg. If you take insulin or certain other diabetes medicines, talk to your diabetes team before skipping meals.

How can I get enough protein when I am not hungry?

Eat protein first at every meal, before the vegetables and starch, and add it to snacks. Soft, easy options such as eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, fish, tofu and lentil soups are often easiest to face. Even half a portion counts. Protein powder is one option some people discuss with a dietitian. If you have kidney disease, check with your doctor before eating more protein.

Why do I feel so full after eating so little?

The medication slows how quickly your stomach empties, so food stays there longer and you feel full sooner. Use a smaller plate, take smaller bites, put your fork down between mouthfuls and stop at the first sign of fullness. Lighter foods sit better than rich, creamy or fried ones. Splitting a meal into two smaller ones an hour or two apart also helps.

What helps with constipation on GLP-1?

Drink more fluids through the day, add fiber gradually from vegetables, beans, lentils, oats and whole grains, and keep gently active, such as a daily walk. Adding lots of fiber at once can cause bloating, so go slowly. If constipation lasts more than a few days or is painful, speak to your pharmacist or doctor rather than starting laxatives on your own.

Can I drink alcohol on GLP-1 medication?

Ask your prescriber, because the answer depends on your medicines and health. Many people find alcohol sits less well and worsens nausea or reflux, and it adds to dehydration. For people taking diabetes medicines, it can raise the risk of low blood sugar, especially on an empty stomach. If you do drink, keep within your country's low-risk guidance and have it with food.

Start with tomorrow's breakfast: put a small portion of protein on a side plate, such as two soft scrambled eggs or a pot of Greek yogurt, eat it slowly and stop when you feel the first sign of fullness. Keep a glass of water in sight all day, and build the rest of your meals the same way.
