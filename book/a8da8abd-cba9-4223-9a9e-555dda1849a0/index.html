Someone you love has died, and the world has not stopped. Here is how to keep breathing through it.

You put the kettle in the fridge. You cannot read a page or remember why you walked into a room. A song in the supermarket leaves you in tears, people say the wrong thing or disappear, there are forms and phone calls you cannot face, and you wonder whether you are grieving wrongly or even going mad. You are not.

Coping With Grief Made Simple is a tender, plain-spoken guide from a former primary school teacher who lost her husband suddenly in her fifties and her mother a few years later after a long illness, and who now volunteers with a bereavement peer support group and a children's grief program. She never tells you how to feel. Instead you learn why grief comes in waves, how to get through the first weeks and the practical tasks, how to ride the hardest moments, help grieving children, handle the people around you, face anniversaries, know when to reach out for more help and slowly let life grow around your loss.
