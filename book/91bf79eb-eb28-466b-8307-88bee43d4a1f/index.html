Cereal for dinner again? You deserve better than that, and it only takes one small pan.

You walk in at seven, the fridge holds two eggs and a bag of salad that has started to sweat, and the delivery menu is already open on your phone. Every recipe you find feeds four, so you eat the same casserole until Sunday. Half an onion dries out in the door, the spinach turns to slime, and every Friday another bag of good food goes in the bin. Slowly you start to believe that cooking for one just is not worth it.

Cooking for One Made Simple is written by a former hotel breakfast cook who spent twenty years feeding crowds, then found herself alone in a quiet kitchen after her children left home and her marriage ended. She lived on toast and cereal for a month and threw away half a bag of spinach every week, until she built a simple system of single portions, small batches, freezer portions and "planned overs." Brisk, witty and warm, she shows you how to scale any recipe down, shop in small amounts, plan one ingredient across three meals and cook 68 savory recipes made for one person, from a two-egg scramble and fried rice for one to lemon chicken thigh with potatoes, red lentil dal, mushroom risotto, little chicken and leek pies and freezer burritos, all with an ordinary kitchen and supermarket food.
