No more five nights of the same stew. Here is how to cook dinner for exactly two, and enjoy every bite.

You cook a recipe for four and eat it on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, until neither of you can face the pot. The chicken comes in packs of four, the cilantro in a bunch big enough for a wedding, and half of it ends up in the bin. Some nights you bump elbows in a tiny kitchen and argue over the knife, and the takeout menu wins again.

The Easy Cooking for Two Cookbook is written by a former bistro line cook who plated 120 dinners a night, then moved into a small apartment with her husband Pete and discovered she could not cook for two without making enough for eight. After a winter of eating one beef stew five nights running, she learned to shop, scale and plan for two. Lively, generous and a little mischievous, she shows you how to halve any recipe, use four right-sized pans, follow a simple weekly rhythm and cook together without bickering, with 66 savory recipes made for two, from shakshuka and Tuscan-style chicken to shrimp tacos, sheet pan gnocchi, a loaf pan lasagna, seared scallops and steak with garlic butter.
