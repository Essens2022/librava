He can forecast every storm in America. He never saw her coming—twice.

Josie Harlan has exactly one summer to save the Starlite , her late granddad's 1956 drive-in on old Route 66. She has a sixty-foot screen held together with zip ties, a three-legged dog who steals hot dogs, a teenage brother she's raising alone, and a slick developer who wants to bulldoze it all for a mega car wash. What she doesn't have is $60,000 —or any patience for the man who left her waiting in that parking lot on graduation night ten years ago.

Wes Callahan is "Weather Wes," America's favorite TV meteorologist—the guy who once ran into a hailstorm on live television to save a kitten (it was a raccoon; the internet forgave him). Now his network has sent him home to Larkspur, Oklahoma, to film a storm-season special… right in the middle of Josie's drive-in.

Then an old photo of the two of them goes viral, the ratings explode, and Wes's producer has an idea: a summer-long, on-camera romance. Fake, of course. Josie gets national publicity for her fundraiser. Wes gets his ratings. They even write the rules on a diner napkin.

Rule 1: No kissing unless the cameras are rolling.

Rule 2: No feelings.

But some fronts can't be stopped. The secret Wes has carried for ten years is heading straight for Josie's heart—and the biggest storm of the summer is lined up for the night of the Starlite Summer Spectacular .
